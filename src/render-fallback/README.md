# src/render-fallback/

The no-WebGL surface: the flat star chart (4.3), semantic DOM and an `aria-hidden` SVG plate, no Three and no canvas. Content parity with the scene is absolute — the artifact payload carries every field this surface renders, because the Fallback admits no truncation. It consumes the same artifact as the scene, which is what keeps the two from ever disagreeing about the data.

**Imports:** `src/shell/` (`panel-model.ts`, `chrome-model.ts`, the contact route `contact-route.tsx` it shares with the Colophon (6.3), and the stylesheet `focus-panel.css` — never the router or `route-link.ts`; the links arrive as props) and `src/tokens/` — the artifact arrives only as the props `App` passes, never by importing `generated/layout.generated.json`. Never `src/data/`, never `src/derive/`, never `src/render-scene/` (dynamically or not), and never Three — neither `three` nor `@react-three/fiber` or `@react-three/drei`, which pull it in.
**Imported by:** `src/shell/fallback-host.tsx`, via dynamic import only.

## Time is the axis

The plate reads one value from the solve, `timeAxis`, and nothing else: no 3D placement, no Body size or band, no Mote tier, no orbit. Those are the scene's projection; the chart is the career's. Every Project marker is drawn at one size and one ink, Deep or Shallow. There are no year marks along the axis: `timeAxis` is a recency rank, so marks spaced along it would lie. Each Client is dated instead (4.5, ruling Q2a) by the artifact's `span` — derived in `src/derive/`, never aggregated here from Project dates — drawn in years (`2016 – 2017`, `2019` when both are equal, `2024 –` while ongoing) as a `chart-label-span` line under the Client's name on both plates.

## Moving between surfaces

The chart's chrome (4.5) is the key screen's `.fb-chrome`: the `<h1>` — `THOMAS NAVARRO · the model`, `the model` an `<a href="/colophon">` — and, opposite it, the route line, which holds `the scene` (`<a href="/">`) where this machine's surface is the scene and nothing on a phone or a machine without WebGL2, who are shown the chart at `/` itself. Both stack at 640px and below. The links are the `links` prop, `{ model, routes }`, each `{ key, text, href, follow }`, built by `src/shell/fallback-host.tsx` from the router and the machine's surface: the chart imports no router or store and reads no surface, and a modified click (a new tab) is the browser's because `follow` says so.

## The Ring alone carries Deep/Shallow

There is no Moon on a flat chart. The Ring, read from the artifact's `isDeep` (never from `content.notable`, never classified here), carries the signal through three redundant structural channels: the ring around the marker on the plate, the same ring glyph in the index's first `Notable` column, and that glyph's accessible name (`Carries a notable` / `No notable`). A two-entry legend under the plates says what the Ring is; it is `aria-hidden`, because it decodes the `aria-hidden` plate and the Notable column already carries the signal for assistive technology. Marker size, brightness, hue and class never carry it, and the records carry no ring (each already shows the Notable or the editorial rule).

## Why the chart is alone on its surface

`App` renders `fallback-host.tsx` alone on the `fallback` surface. The DOM destination index's buttons are scene doors (fly, pull back, focus a panel), and the corner chrome and the Focus panel are scene chrome; on the chart, the index table and the record anchors are the destination index, and none of it is canvas-backed. The page scrolls natively: `src/shell/page.css` locks scrolling only while the scene host is in the document.
