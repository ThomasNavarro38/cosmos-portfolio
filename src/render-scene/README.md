# src/render-scene/

The 3D surface: React Three Fiber, drei, three. Reads solved positions and derived visual properties from the artifact and renders them. Contains no classification test and no data-to-visual mapping — those live in `src/derive/`.

**Imports:** `generated/layout.generated.json`, `src/tokens/`, and `src/shell/` state. Never `src/data/`, never `src/derive/`, never the solver's `d3-force-3d`, and never `src/render-fallback/` — the two surfaces are alternatives, so neither loads the other, dynamically or not.
**Imported by:** `src/shell/` via dynamic import only, so Three stays off the entry path.

## The scene reads, and never derives

Every value this surface draws was resolved in `src/derive/` and emitted into the artifact (AD-3). Positions arrive solved; `mass`, `luminanceBand`, `railDistance`, `moteTier` and `isDeep` arrive resolved. Nothing here classifies a Project, recomputes a mapping rule, branches on a raw `content` field, or runs a force simulation — and `scene-model.ts` imports **nothing at all**, so that is structural rather than asserted. A renderer that re-derived any of them would put that rule in a second place, and the two would drift the first time either changed. `neverPackages: ['d3-force-3d']` on this directory's boundary row makes "imports nothing from the solver" enforced rather than declared.

**A Company is an attribute, never a Body.** `content.company` names who the work was done through; it adds no entity, so it adds no mesh. **The only lines are the orbits and the Rail** (3.2) — never a leader, edge or link between two Bodies. Both are drei `<Line>`s, drawn in `body-field.tsx` alone and fed only from named model functions: `sceneLines` (each orbit is `orbitPoints`, the circle through the Planet in the plane the artifact's `orbitNormal` names — the orbit solved, never reconstructed) and `railLine` (a Catmull-Rom through the stop Stars' centres, not the camera's path, which the camera rides and so could never see; no line under two stops). Every other module here still draws no line: `auditConnectors` covers drei's `Line`, `QuadraticBezierLine`, `CubicBezierLine` and `Segments` by name as well as Three's `lineSegments` intrinsics, because a scan matching lowercase intrinsics alone passed a reconstructed `<Line points={…} />` at full green; `auditBodyField` holds the one file allowed a `<Line>` to exactly two, each bound to the model.

## The stroke and ink ladders (3.2)

Every scene line takes one of the five `stroke-*-dark` tokens and a `components.stroke-weight` weight; every scene glyph takes an `ink-*-dark` token; neither ever borrows the other's (`auditLadders`). A Star at rest is `stroke-rest`; a Planet at rest is its derived `luminanceBand` (`stroke-rest` or `stroke-near` — so a recent Shallow Planet is bright *for a resting Body* and never white, and no Body is drawn at the Rail's and the Dust's `stroke-dust`). `stroke-focus` is spent once per frame on `sceneSubject`'s one Body, and `stroke-active` is a Body's only as the approach (3.4, below): the one approached Planet, at `hairline`, unless it is the subject. Bodies are camera-facing `fill: none` circles with a filled core dot on Stars; there are **no lights** — every material is unlit — and no shadow, blur, glow, bloom or radius anywhere in this directory or `src/shell/` (a tree scan, comments stripped). Depth is the ladder plus the vignette: a full-frame radial gradient of `vignette-dark`, 0 inside 42% rising to 0.9 at the corners (sized to the farthest corner, so each edge's midpoint stays below 0.9), written with `color-mix` so no literal colour appears. The quiet ink step fails the contrast floor and is named nowhere under `src/` outside `src/tokens/`.

Closed in the 3.9 audit (ruling 4b-i): DESIGN.md §2 says `stroke-dust` is "not used for any Body", and derive's `luminanceBand` used to emit it for the oldest Projects. The band is now two steps, `stroke-rest` and `stroke-near` (`RESTING_STEPS`, pinned equal to derive's `RESTING_BANDS`), so the oldest Planets equal their `stroke-rest` orbit; `bodyStroke` throws on a `stroke-dust` Planet. The Rail, the Dust and the Mote orbit lines stay at `stroke-dust`.

## The Deep/Shallow signal: Moon and Ring (3.3)

A visitor must be able to tell which Projects carry a Notable without opening any of them, so every Deep Planet carries two layered structural marks, at rest and always (DESIGN.md §7.2): a **Ring** — a continuous concentric circle at `stroke-near`, `hairline` (0.7px), 2.2 × the body radius, drawn in the Body's own billboard by `body-field.tsx` — and a **Moon** — a filled dot at `stroke-active`, 0.27 × the body radius, centred 2 × the body radius out at the fixed billboard-local angle π/12 (up-right), drawn by `moon-field.tsx`. The Moon crosses the Ring rather than sitting inside or beyond it: centred at 2 × r with radius 0.27 × r, its disc spans 1.73–2.27 × r along its direction, so the 2.2 × r Ring runs through it near its outer edge (as in the direction-03 mock); when the pixel floor enlarges it, the disc grows about the same centre and still straddles the Ring. The Moon draws at `MOON_RENDER_ORDER`, one above the Ring's `BODY_RENDER_ORDER`, so the overlap is always Moon-over-Ring and never left to three's depth sort, which would flicker as the camera moves. A Shallow Planet and a Star carry neither. The Ring means the Project carries a Notable — nothing more; every Project can be focused, and no caption, label or comment string anywhere explains a mark (a scan over every non-test source under `src/` holds that for the Ring).

**`isDeep` alone.** `notable-model.ts` holds every rule and decides on a Body's `kind` and its artifact `isDeep` alone — it also carries the Body's `id` and `position` through to key and place the marks, and takes the size from `bodyRadius` — and nothing else enters the decision: never the band, the mass, the subject or the navigation state — so every Deep Planet of a system shows both marks at once, and focusing one (its outline goes white, per 3.2) leaves its Ring and Moon unchanged — and never `notable` or a `content` field, since the derive layer already classified the Project (AD-3). **Size and brightness are not the signal**: they are bound to duration and recency (FR-3), so `bodyStroke` and `bodyRadius` give a Planet the same outline whatever its `isDeep`. `notable-model.test.ts` executes that over every Planet of the committed artifact and every reachable subject, with `isDeep` flipped. The Ring at `stroke-near` on a recent Planet whose band is also `stroke-near` is the same token, and legal: the Ring is a structural channel, distinct by geometry (2.2 × r), never by tone.

**The Moon's pixel floor.** At a client-stop distance a 0.27 × r dot can fall under a resolvable size, so each frame `moon-field.tsx` scales the Moon's mesh by `moonScale`: `max(1, 2.4 · worldPerPixel / radius)`, with `worldPerPixel = 2·depth / (P[5] · heightPx)`, `depth` the Planet's view-space depth read from three's column-major view matrix (`−(V[2]x + V[6]y + V[10]z + V[14])`) and `P[5] = 1/tan(fov/2)`. The scale is applied about the Moon's own centre, so it never moves along its direction. Behind the camera (`depth ≤ 0`) or on any non-finite input the scale is 1. With the floor, the Moon is the mark that stays resolvable at any distance; the Ring is the larger structural mark — 2.2 × r across at a client stop, a 0.7px line that reads as a circle on a low-DPI screen or a compressed capture where a few-pixel dot reads as noise.

Both marks face the camera through drei's `<Billboard>`, are unlit, and draw in a `transparent` material at opacity 1 after the Dust, like every Body draw — the Ring at `BODY_RENDER_ORDER`, the Moon at `MOON_RENDER_ORDER`. `<MoonField>` is mounted once inside the Canvas after `<BodyField>`, so its frame callback runs after the rig has moved the camera. It reads no store and holds no state.

## Affordance (3.4)

Approaching a Project teaches what it is before it is opened. **One approached Project per frame** (`approachedProject` in `affordance-model.ts`): `keyboardProjectId ?? hoveredProjectId`, and `null` when that is the Focused Project. The keyboard's field is written by the index's Project buttons only on a `:focus-visible` focus, through the doors `approachByKeyboard` / `leaveByKeyboard` (`src/shell/activate.ts`); the pointer's by the hit spheres' `onPointerOver` / `onPointerOut`, through `hoverBody` / `leaveBody` (`scene-pick.ts`) — Planets only, never a touch (a tap's out may never come), and a leave clears only when it still names the current holder, so a stale out never erases the next over. R3F hit-tests only when the pointer moves, so a camera moved by the wheel, a flight or Focus would leave the approach on a Planet that slid away: each frame, before it draws, `<AffordanceField>` calls `repickPointer`, which re-dispatches R3F's last pointer event while the pointer is over the canvas (never after it has left — R3F keeps that event — so nothing is approached under a pointer on the index or the panel). R3F fires over/out only on a change, so a still camera writes nothing. Hover reveals and never activates: neither binding calls a door.

The approached Planet gets one straight leader — a single open `M … L …` SVG `<path>` at `stroke-rest`, `hairline-leader` (0.6px), from its screen edge — on a Deep Planet from beyond its Ring and Moon (`markPx`), so it never cuts the 3.3 signal — to the label anchor, 56px — and a two-line label: the title at `scene-label-project` in `ink-lift`, and `{role} · team of {team_size} · {Deep|Shallow}` (the index's own `DEPTH_LABELS`, off `isDeep`) at `scene-label-secondary` in `ink-label`. Its outline steps to `stroke-active` at `hairline` (`bodyStroke`'s third argument), unless it is the subject, which keeps `stroke-focus`. The keyboard's approach is identical plus a 1px `stroke-focus` square outline at a 3px offset around the label block — never the Body; that outline beside a white subject is the one sanctioned exception to "one `stroke-focus` per frame". No ring, halo or new geometry around a Body, no scale, fade or hue, no tooltip and no live region.

The label sits right of the Body; when its block — its size estimated from the text and the type roles (`labelBox`), never measured — would cross the outer 12% of the frame on an axis, it re-anchors toward the centre on that axis (left of the Body at the right edge, below it at the top, above it at the bottom), and the anchor is then held so the whole block lies inside the inner 76% (`labelBounds`); only a Body in the outer band gets a longer leader. While a Project is Focused the panel's edge is the right bound instead, as for the Motes' labels: the overlay is under the panel. Projection is `ndc = P·V·p` and the Body's pixel radius 3.3's `r / worldPerPixel`; a Planet behind the camera, or in front of it with its projected centre off the canvas, hides the leader and the label (the overlay stays mounted, and the Planet's `stroke-active` outline step is `<BodyField>`'s, unaffected). `<AffordanceField>` (mounted last in the Canvas) writes each frame's result onto four refs inside a drei `<Html fullscreen>` pinned to the canvas's top-left, at `zIndexRange={[1, 1]}` — over the vignette, under the panel — `aria-hidden`, with no pointer events. It reads the store once per frame and writes no field itself; its re-pick (`repickPointer`) reaches `hoveredProjectId` only through `hoverBody` / `leaveBody`, as a pointer move would.

## Mote tiers and label legibility (3.5)

A Mote's orbit is its Skill's artifact tier: `mote-model.ts` reads `skills` as a lookup (`moteRanks`) and never counts shares — tier index is orbit index, and among a Project's Skills on one orbit the `j`-th by rank sits at slot `2j` of `max(MOTE_MIN_SLOTS, 2k)`. A Skill the list does not carry throws naming it. Each dot is drawn at a screen-constant 2.6 / 2.1 / 1.8px by tier (`worldPerPixel`, shared with the Moon's floor in `notable-model.ts`) times the level scale, filled `stroke-active` (inner) or `ink-label` (DESIGN.md `mote`). Each orbit has one closed `stroke-dust` hairline circle on its tilt, writing no depth and built once, drawn only while a Mote is on that orbit: at the highest level of any Mote on it, condensing or decaying, around that Mote's Planet (a tie goes to the condensing Mote). On an `A → B` hand-off a line therefore moves to whichever Planet holds the brighter Mote on its orbit, in one frame — accepted in review (2026-10-10) over a second, fading line per orbit. They are the only lines `mote-field.tsx` draws, held to one block by `auditMoteField`. `moteRanks` is memoised per artifact object and throws naming a Skill listed twice. `MOTE_ORBIT_PHASES` moved to `[0, π/4, π/2]` so the nine-Skill fixture's labels all read at rest at 1440×900.

Labels are the `mote-label` token in `ink-label`; the width estimate is `chars × 9.5 × (0.6 + 0.16)` ≈ 7.2px and the box `9.5 × 1.2` tall — conservative and tunable. After panel blocking (unchanged), `moteLabelPass` shows a label only while fewer than `MOTE_LABEL_LIMIT` (12) are shown, its box is inside the canvas on all four edges and left of the panel, and it clears every shown label by `MOTE_LABEL_CLEARANCE_PX`; it visits the focused Project's Motes first, in rank order, so the most-shared Skills win. A label it hides is not re-slotted. The cap and the order live in that one function, so a later degradation rule replaces it whole. The panel lists every Skill in the DOM (`src/shell/`), so a capped label loses no information. The tested maximum is 25 Skills (`sncf/client-referential`).

## Why the model and the renderer are separate files

`node --test` under Node 24's built-in type stripping cannot load a `.tsx` file **in any position** — `ERR_UNKNOWN_FILE_EXTENSION` at module resolution, before any transform, so a `.ts` test cannot even import a `.tsx` module. Adding a transform means adding dependencies to a set deliberately closed at fifteen exactly-pinned versions.

So every rule an acceptance criterion names lives in `scene-model.ts`: the flattening, the identity guarantees, the ordering, both guards, and the framing. `three` itself *does* load under the built-in runner, so frustum containment is executed too — a real `PerspectiveCamera` at the framing's position and rotation, and `Frustum.containsPoint` for every Body. What is left in `scene.tsx` is the mesh mapping, which is the part no unit test would meaningfully assert anyway: scene correctness is perceptual and is tested by looking at it. That residual is ratcheted — `scene-model.test.ts` scans the renderer and fails if it grows a sort, a filter, a hook, a store write, a re-derivation, or a connector.

## Dust never reads as fog

Dust (FR-15) is two components computed in `dust-model.ts` from the Body list alone: a broad radial **medium** centred on each Client's Star, and sparse **particles** between Bodies. It is composition read off solved positions — the same kind of work as `frameBodies` — not a data-to-visual mapping, so it lives here rather than in `src/derive/`. It reads no `content`, no Skill and no authored field; if Dust ever encodes Skills per Client, that becomes a derivation and moves to `src/derive/`. Density is a ratio (`DUST_PARTICLES_PER_BODY`) plus a peak opacity (`DUST_MEDIUM_PEAK_OPACITY`, 0.42 per DESIGN.md §7.5): retuning either is a one-constant edit. The scatter is seeded, so it is identical across builds and renders. The default framing still frames Bodies only.

"A Body's luminance step is ambiguous against the Dust" is perceptual, so it is checked by looking (the Dust sits at `stroke-dust`, the bottom of the ladder every Body is drawn on). What *can* be executed is each way Dust could make a Body ambiguous, and each has its own proxy:

- **Painting over a Body — draw order.** Every Dust draw carries `DUST_RENDER_ORDER`, every Body draw (outline, core dot, orbit, Rail — all in `body-field.tsx`) `BODY_RENDER_ORDER`, and the first is below the second. Dust writes no depth, so a Body drawn afterwards always passes the depth test and wins, whether the Dust is in front of it or behind it, from any camera 2.5 introduces. **The Bodies are `transparent` at opacity 1, and that is load-bearing:** three renders its whole opaque list before its transparent list whatever the `renderOrder`, so an opaque Body would be drawn *before* the (transparent) Dust and then painted over. Moving the Bodies into the sorted transparent pass is what makes `renderOrder` decide. `scene-model.test.ts` scans both files for all of this (`auditDrawOrder` over every `media` and `particles` block of `scene.tsx`, `auditBodyField` over every draw of `body-field.tsx`) and is driven over mutations of the shipped files with each guarantee removed. Because no Dust fragment — medium or particle — ever alters a Body's own pixels, the particles' opacity sits outside the medium's ceiling: the ceiling bounds what stacks *behind* a Body, and particles never pile up there (see clearance below).
- **Piling up behind a Body — non-overlapping media, in world space.** Each medium's radius is capped at half the distance to the nearest other Star, so two media's spheres of extent can at most touch where both have fallen to zero, and the composite medium alpha at any point in the scene — every Body included — is at most `DUST_MEDIUM_PEAK_OPACITY`. This is a guarantee over sphere extents, **not over the screen**: the media are camera-facing sprites, so when the view runs along the line joining two systems their sprites can still stack on screen. The cap has a second cost: where two Stars are close, a medium can be *smaller than its own system*, leaving its outer Planets beyond its gradient. Non-overlap wins over coverage by design.
- **Crowding a Body — clearance.** No particle lands within `BODY_MARGIN` of any Body centre, and `BODY_MARGIN` covers the largest Body radius plus a particle's, so the clearance holds between surfaces rather than only between centres. Placement attempts are bounded, so a crowded layout yields a short scatter rather than a hang or a crowded Body.

The final judgement is perceptual and is re-asked when real data lands: the committed artifact holds one Client and one Project, so the neighbouring-systems claims are proven over in-memory fixtures.

The medium is a billboard `sprite`. Its texture is one module-level `DataTexture`, uploaded once and shared by every medium, whose texels come from `mediumFalloff`: white RGBA, with alpha falling from 255 at the centre to 0 at the edge. The sprite is scaled by `mediumSpriteScale`, which accounts for the falloff reaching 0 half a texel inside the quad, so each medium fades out exactly at its model radius and the non-overlap proof above holds for what is drawn. `auditDrawOrder` requires that scale by name. The texture is tinted by `DUST_MEDIUM_COLOR` (`stroke-rest-dark`), and the particles take `DUST_PARTICLE_COLOR` (`stroke-dust-dark`), both read from `src/tokens/tokens.ts` and drawn on the scene's `surface-base-dark` background.

## Why the canvas root is `aria-hidden="true"`

The DOM destination index is the interface (AD-4). This surface draws the same destinations a second time, and narrating them twice is worse than not narrating them once: a screen-reader user would walk a list of destinations, then walk a WebGL canvas that announces nothing useful about the same ones. So the canvas subtree is hidden from the accessibility tree and every destination stays reachable through the index.

**The attribute lands on R3F's wrapper `<div>`, not on the `<canvas>`.** `CanvasProps extends React.HTMLAttributes<HTMLDivElement>`, so every DOM attribute written on `<Canvas>` goes to the wrapper R3F mounts. That satisfies the rule rather than departing from it — `aria-hidden` applies to a subtree, so the `<canvas>` and anything R3F places beside it are hidden together — and the wrapper *is* the canvas root. Verified in a browser against both the dev server and the production build: walking up from the rendered `<canvas>` reaches `aria-hidden="true"`, and the document's only focusable elements are the index's buttons. The scan that holds it asserts the attribute's **value**, not its presence: `aria-hidden="false"` and `aria-hidden={false}` both passed a presence-keyed scan at full green.

## The surface has extent, and the camera is aimed

R3F's wrapper is `width: 100%; height: 100%`, and this repo holds no CSS at all — so a `<Canvas>` mounted in a bare `<main>` collapses to zero height and draws nothing. That shipped once and was caught in a browser rather than by a test. The canvas therefore carries an inline size, and the default camera is computed from the Bodies rather than hard-coded, because the solver decides where they land: a constant distance framed the one committed Planet by luck and would lose the first Body solved beyond it. Neither is the visual identity Epic 3 owns — colour, tokens and typography are deferred; whether the surface *exists* and whether the Bodies are inside the frustum are this story's own acceptance.

**The camera's orientation is passed, never inherited.** React Three Fiber calls `camera.lookAt(0, 0, 0)` whenever the `camera` prop carries no `rotation` (`@react-three/fiber/dist/events-156d8d12.esm.js:15786`). A framing that returned a position and a target and trusted the default therefore aimed at the **world origin** rather than at the Bodies' centre, and the committed Planet at `x: 10` fell outside the frustum with all 488 tests green. So `frameBodies` returns an explicit `rotation` of `[0, 0, 0]` and stands the camera on the `+z` side at the centre's own `x` and `y` — an unrotated Three camera looks down `-z`, so looking down `-z` *is* looking at the centre, and there is no target left for a dependency to choose. `near` and `far` are passed for the same reason.

Containment is asserted through a **real** `three.PerspectiveCamera` built at that position and rotation, never by re-deriving the projection. Axis-aligned arithmetic around the camera's own `x`/`y` is valid only if the camera looks down `-z`, so it cannot observe the orientation being wrong — which is precisely how this shipped off-frame twice.

`NARROWEST_ASPECT` is why the fit takes the larger of the vertical and horizontal distances: Three derives the horizontal field of view from the vertical one times the viewport aspect, so a portrait viewport narrows the `x` axis — the axis the Rail runs along. A vertical-only fit lost the outermost Body at 390 × 844.

## The Rail: one trajectory, one warp, two damped scalars

The camera travels the Rail backward through time (FR-16, FR-17, UX-DR30). `rail-model.ts` resolves the keyframes: **keyframe 0 is the opening frame** (`frameBodies`, the whole career First Paint already shows) and **keyframes 1..N are the Client stops, present first** — `rail` read from the end, exactly as `src/shell/index-model.ts` reads it, so the camera and the index walk one sequence. Stops are Clients only (AD-4a); a Project is never a stop. A keyframe is its Shot's subject (the Client's Star, by ID) plus the Shot's relative `angle` (elevation above the orbital plane) and `distance`, plus its Dwell tempo, so **no world coordinate appears in the Rail**: translating every Body by `v` translates every keyframe by `v`, and a coordinate-literal scan holds both Rail files to that. A rail Client with no Shot, no Star, no Dwell entry or an unknown tempo throws naming the ID; the scene's error boundary keeps the index.

**A stop frames its whole system at any aspect** (FR-17a). At the live aspect a stop stands at `max(shot.distance, contain(aspect))` along the Shot's direction. `containDistance` is closed-form and exact: each Body of the Client (its Star and every Planet with that `clientId`), as a sphere of `STOP_MARGIN`, needs `d ≥ δ + (|u|·cos H + m)/sin H` for the horizontal half-fov `H`, the same with `v` and the vertical half-fov `V`, and `d ≥ δ + m + near`, where δ is its offset toward the camera and `u`, `v` its lateral offsets; the maximum over the Bodies is the answer. `H` comes from `DEFAULT_FOV` and the aspect (floored at `NARROWEST_ASPECT`), so it is continuous in the aspect and never grows as the viewport widens. The Shot's angle always wins and its distance wins when larger, so a Shot overrides the default without cropping a Project out. `rail-model.test.ts` checks every stop against a real `three` frustum densely across aspects 0.45–3, then at 4, 10 and 1000, with a negative control that crops; the fixtures put Planets beside, toward the camera from and below their Star, and include a Star-only Client. The near-plane bound never decides at `DEFAULT_FOV` 50 — the vertical bound alone exceeds it — and stays as a guard for a wider fov or a deeper near plane. `STOP_MARGIN` must cover the largest drawn radius, asserted in `scene-model.test.ts`.

**Dwell is scroll travel, not time** (FR-18, AD-4a). Each stop's slowdown `k` is its artifact tempo through `DWELL_SLOWDOWN` (`dwell-brief < dwell-steady < dwell-held`, every value in `[0, 1)` and at most 0.85), looked up by Client ID; the opening frame has `RAIL_OPENING_SLOWDOWN`. No clock, no hold timer, no snap: a held stop is crossed more slowly, so equal wheel travel keeps the camera near it for longer.

`progress ∈ [0, N]` is measured in stops. On each segment it is warped by `s = p − [k_a·sin(2πt) + Δk·(t·sin(2πt) + (cos(2πt) − 1)/2π)]/2π` with `t = p − ⌊p⌋`: the integral of the rate `1 − k(t)·cos(2πt)`, with `k` blended linearly from one stop's slowdown to the next. It fixes every integer, its rate at stop `i` is `1 − k_i` from both sides (C1), and it is at least `1 − max k > 0` everywhere — a slowdown, never a halt. `s` drives two curves, both C1 at every knot: a uniform Catmull-Rom through the keyframes' aims, and a monotone cubic (Fritsch–Carlson) through their offsets, with the camera at aim + offset. The offset curve cannot overshoot, and every keyframe offset has `z > 0` — a Shot's angle must lie strictly inside `±π/2` — so the camera never crosses behind its aim. A Catmull-Rom on the positions themselves did cross, on a wide five-Client fixture, and a sampled test now holds `position.z − target.z > 0` along the whole path. The rig damps two scalars, progress and aspect, with a critically damped spring each (`RAIL_SPRING_SECONDS`, solved exactly, `dt` clamped to `RAIL_MAX_FRAME_SECONDS`); the trajectory is evaluated at the damped values and the pose itself is never damped, so the camera's lag is the spring's alone. Velocity lives in the spring state, so a new target mid-flight bends the motion rather than restarting it. A resize reframes continuously: a stop's distance is its containment at the damped aspect, which reads zero and below as the floor, so an aspect spring overshooting a sliver goal cannot cut, and a collapsed canvas cannot fling the camera away. The opening frame keeps its own reach at every aspect, so progress 0 is always exactly the `camera` prop. The monotone cubic treats a knot-to-knot delta at rounding scale — judged against that pair's own values, so a far Shot elsewhere cannot flatten a real small turn — as flat, so two stops with equal framing stay translation-invariant after a re-solve. Every stop is seen from `RAIL_HEADING`, which must stay strictly inside `±π/2`. The rig's first frame starts at rest at progress 0, the frame the prop already shows, and springs out to whatever the store holds, so a remount never cuts. Because a narrow stop stands further out than the opening frame, the prop's `far` is `max(framing.far, railFarPlane(rail, bodies))`, which samples a grid of progress by aspect (floor to `DESKTOP_ASPECT` densely, then twice that and `+∞`) and adds half the grid's longest chord as slack for what falls between samples; a test sweeps aspects far more densely than the model does, out to `+∞`, and holds the plane within 25% of the real need.

Not here: containment along the path between stops, which nothing proves yet. (Motes and Condensation, free flight and reduced motion are all below.) Reduced motion (2.10, below) shortens the spring's time constant to `RAIL_SPRING_SECONDS × pace`; the Dwell warp and every rest pose are untouched.

## Free flight: a layer between the Rail and Focus (2.9)

The camera composes three layers every frame: `rail → flight → focus`. The Rail's springs keep running in free flight, because flight never writes `scrollProgress` and the return must land where the Rail is now. `flightFrame` decides the pose beneath Focus: the Rail's own on the Rail, or the flight goal in free flight — a Client's stop, or the whole universe (`flightTarget: null`). `focusFrame` receives that composed frame, so releasing Focus in free flight lands on the flight pose.

**Flight adds no framing.** `railPose` at an integer `k` is keyframe `k` at the live aspect, so the flight pose for a Client is exactly how the Rail frames it at its stop — containment included (FR-17a) — and the overview is the opening frame, which frames every Body. No new Shot and no coordinate: the stop is found by the Client's ID among the keyframes' subjects. The camera prop's far plane already covers it (`railFarPlane` samples every stop, and a blend of two poses is never further from a Body than the further of them).

**One move.** A change of goal key — the mode, or the target in free flight — captures the drawn pose and eases from it to the new goal over `FLIGHT_SECONDS` (greybox) on `easeFocus`, linear in position and aim as in `stepFocus`. A retarget mid-flight restarts from the drawn pose, so nothing cuts; the goal is re-read every frame, so the return eases to the live Rail pose. The first frame is at rest on the Rail pose, easing on from there when the store is already in free flight.

## Focus: one pose, one eased move (2.7)

The Focus panel is a fixed column over the right of a full-width canvas, covering the share `w` of its width, so the free region in NDC is `[−1, 1 − 2w]` and its centre is `−w`. `focusPose` looks along the Project's Client stop `direction` and shifts its aim along the camera's right axis by `d·tan(H)·w`, which lands the Planet at NDC `−w`; `d` is the largest of the closed-form bounds that keep a `FOCUS_REACH` sphere inside each side of the free region, inside the vertical field and in front of `DEFAULT_NEAR`. No coordinate literal appears, so a re-solve moves the pose with its Planet.

`stepFocus` is the whole transition. A change of the focused ID captures the pose drawn last frame and eases from it to the new goal over `FOCUS_SECONDS` on `easeFocus` — the duration and curve the panel's CSS slide uses — so an interruption restarts from where the camera is and nothing cuts. The motion carries a mirror of the panel's slide (`FocusSlide`: open, elapsed, length, factor). A change that opens or closes the panel while that slide is still running is a CSS reversal: the browser runs the panel's reverse for `FOCUS_SECONDS × reversingFactor`, and the camera's ease takes exactly that long, so a quick release still ends as one move. `A → B` leaves the panel open, so its slide runs on and the camera takes the full duration. The goal on release is the live Rail pose; the Rail's springs keep running underneath, and with nothing focused the Rail pose is drawn exactly. The rig starts at most one frame behind the store change.

## Condensation and decay: one Mote per Skill, on the Focus move (2.8)

A focused Project's Skills condense out of its Client's Dust onto three orbits around its Planet, and decay back when Focus moves on or is released. `mote-model.ts` steps one state **per Skill name**, and `mote-field.tsx` keeps one pooled dot and one label per distinct name in the artifact, so a Skill can never be two Motes and the `A → B` hand-off of a shared Skill is one object moving.

**One blend, on the Focus move.** `blendMote(from, to, fromLevel, toLevel, elapsed)` is condensation (source → slot, 0 → 1), decay (drawn pose → source, → 0) and hand-off (drawn pose → B's slot, staying at 1), over `FOCUS_SECONDS` on `easeFocus` — the camera's and the panel's duration and curve, so "B condenses as it arrives" holds by construction. Any change of the focused ID captures the drawn pose and level, so nothing cuts; `FOCUS_EASING` is symmetric, so a decay toward a static source retraces its condensation. `A → B` runs A-only decay, the shared hand-off and B-only condensation concurrently. A Mote at level 0 at the end of a decay is removed.

**Sources are visible Dust.** A Mote starts and ends at its source: its slot pushed outward from the Planet by `MOTE_SOURCE_SPREAD`, then pulled radially toward the Star into the ball where `mediumAlphaAt ≥ MOTE_SOURCE_ALPHA` — `radius · √(1 − √(α / peak))`, the closed-form inverse of `(1 − t²)²`.

**Orbits are the artifact's Skill tier (3.5; until then greybox list order).** Each Skill sits on its tier's orbit, the `j`-th by rank at slot `2j` of `max(8, 2k)`, so no two Motes of one Planet ever sit on adjacent slots of one orbit — see "Mote tiers and label legibility" above. The three radii (outermost plus the dot inside `FOCUS_REACH`, innermost clear of the largest Body) are tilted −16°, +26° and −46° about world `x`, and turn at `MOTE_ORBIT_RADIANS_PER_SECOND` so condensed Motes keep moving while the panel is read. Nothing here reads `moteTier`.

**Labels never sit under the panel.** Placement is a pure function of NDC positions, projected through the camera matrices the frame passes in (`projectNdc`, checked against `three`'s own projection). A label reads left of its dot when the dot is left of the Planet on screen, right otherwise. While focused, a right-hand label whose estimated width (`chars × MOTE_LABEL_CHAR_PX`, from the `mote-label` sans token: `9.5 × (0.6 + 0.16)` ≈ 7.2px, wide on average but not for every string) crosses `1 − 2w` is hidden that frame, never truncated. Blocking is judged at the Mote's destination slot, not at its in-flight pose (a source pushed outward, or a hand-off between Planets); `pose` blends toward the live, gliding slot, so an in-flight re-slot bends the path, never cuts it; a blocked Mote is re-slotted on the next step, in flight or condensed (never a decaying one): its angle eases over `MOTE_RESLOT_SECONDS` to the nearest slot of its orbit that is free, keeps the no-adjacency quota and would not be blocked. When no slot qualifies — a crowded orbit, with every free slot beside an occupied one — the Mote stays where it is and its label stays hidden. After placement, `moteLabelPass` (3.5) shows at most `MOTE_LABEL_LIMIT` labels — the focused Project's first, then by rank — each inside the four canvas edges and left of the panel (still, while Motes decay after a release, since the panel slides out over the same move), clear of every shown label by `MOTE_LABEL_CLEARANCE_PX`; a label it hides is not re-slotted, and the panel's Skills list carries every Skill.

**The binding.** `<MoteField>` is mounted after `<RailCamera>` inside the `<Canvas>`, so R3F runs its frame callback after the rig has moved the camera; it calls `camera.updateMatrixWorld()`, reads `navigationStore.getState()` once, writes no store field, and applies each draw with `applyMoteDraw` and each orbit draw with `applyMoteOrbitDraw`. Dots draw at `BODY_RENDER_ORDER`, transparent, after the Dust; labels are drei `<Html>` at `zIndexRange={[1, 1]}` (above the vignette, below the panel) with no pointer events, inside the `aria-hidden` canvas root — no live region. No connector and no hover or click on a Mote; the only lines are the three orbit lines (3.5). drei's `<Html>` projects its own position in its own frame callback, which R3F runs before this one (a child subscribes first), so a label trails its dot by one frame during a camera move.

## The scene writes only through the shell's doors, and reacts to the Rail, to flight and to Focus

One interaction in this directory: a Body's click, on its hidden hit sphere in `body-field.tsx`, bound to `activateBody` (`scene-pick.ts`) — a Star flies to its Client through `activateClient`, a Planet enters Focus through `activateProject` (flying to its Client first in free flight), a drag past `PICK_DRAG_PX` or a multi-click's later click does nothing, and the click stops at the nearest Body. Since 3.4 a Planet's hit sphere also takes the pointer's over and out (`hoverBody` / `leaveBody`), which write `hoveredProjectId` through `hoverProject` and reveal its affordance — hover never activates. No pointer-missed (a click on the void changes nothing), no drag or orbit, and nothing on a Mote; `auditPick` allows exactly those bindings inside the Bodies' map and rejects every neighbour of it; `auditRenderer` holds `scene.tsx` to no handler at all. Dwell is a pace in the Rail model, not a reaction. The only motion is the rig and the Motes (2.8), and the inputs they read are `scrollProgress`, which the shell's wheel and key handlers in `src/shell/scene-host.tsx` write (their pure rules are `src/shell/rail-input.ts`'s; `Home` goes through the `goHome` door), `mode` and `flightTarget` (the rig), and `focusedProjectId`. The Dust itself does not animate: no drift.

**Reduced motion (2.10).** The rig and the Mote field read `reducedMotion()` (`src/shell/motion-preference.ts`) once per frame — never subscribed — and hand it to every frame function as a trailing `reduced = false`: `railFrame`, `flightFrame`, `focusFrame`, `moteFrame`. Reduced, the Rail spring runs on `RAIL_SPRING_SECONDS × pace`, `stepFlight`, `stepFocus` and `stepMotes` advance their elapsed time by `clamp(dt) / pace` after the existing clamp — so each eased move completes at exactly `duration × pace`, continuously, and Focus and Condensation shorten identically — and the Mote orbit clock does not advance, so the Motes hold their angles. Dwell (`warpProgress`) and every rest pose are untouched. The models import only `motionPace`; an audit in `scene-model.test.ts` pins which files may reference the preference at all, each in its role.

**No other write to the navigation store, ever** — no `setState` and no store event named here: the only writes are the two doors `activateClient` / `activateProject`, called from `activateBody`, and the pointer's approach `hoverProject`, called from `hoverBody` / `leaveBody` (3.4), and nowhere else in this directory. `src/shell/state.ts` is the one authority on where the visitor is (AD-9) and every transition is a named event; a `setState` from a renderer would be a transition nobody can find. A tree-wide scan in `state.test.ts` enforces that for every shipped file under `src/`, this directory included.

**And no reactive binding in a frame callback.** `rail-camera.tsx` is that callback, and `scene-model.test.ts` holds it with its own audit (`auditRig`, driven over mutations of the shipped rig): no store write, no named event, no subscription or reactive hook, no DOM, no coordinate literal, and — positively — a frame loop that is *exactly* one `getState()` read, `railFrame(motion.current, rail, state.scrollProgress, size.width, size.height, delta)` with the motion written back, `flightFrame(flight.current, rail, state.mode, state.flightTarget, frame, delta)` with its motion written back, `focusFrame(focus.current, rail, bodies, state.focusedProjectId, flown.frame, size.width, delta)` written back, and Focus's pose applied. `railFrame`, `flightFrame` and `focusFrame` are executed in `rail-model.test.ts`, `flight-model.test.ts` and `focus-model.test.ts`, so the binding is the only thing left to read. A tree scan also fails if anything outside this directory imports `rail-model.ts` or `rail-camera.tsx`. A camera subscribed with a hook would re-render its whole tree on every progress write, sixty times a second, to obtain a value it is about to read anyway; the path is `navigationStore.getState()` inside the callback. That rule is held by a **source scan** rather than by a boundary row, and the reason is worth stating: `packageOf` in `src/derive/boundaries.ts` reduces every bare specifier to its package, so `zustand/react` and `zustand/vanilla` are both `zustand` — a `neverPackages: ['zustand']` row here would ban the navigation store this file's import line explicitly permits. So `scene-model.test.ts` scans for a `zustand/react` import, for `useStore`, and for `useFrame` outside the rig, and `boundaries.test.ts` asserts the row is absent so re-adding it fails with this explanation attached. The scan is narrower than the row would have been: it names `scene.tsx` and `rail-camera.tsx` and the forms listed (the rig's audit adds `subscribe`, `useSyncExternalStore` and `useShallow`), not every route to a subscription — `deferred-work.md` records what it does not reach.
