# src/render-scene/realism/

The realism layer's home, and the `realism` chunk (7.5). The chunk decodes the maps the artifact names, draws one fill per Planet from them (7.6), and draws one fill and one glow per Star (7.7), draws every orbit's debris belt in one mesh (9.6), draws the Rail as one nebula ribbon (9.7), and draws a ring disc and a moon sphere per Deep Planet (9.8). Once the maps have decoded, the layer is uploaded and compiled off-screen, drawn once hidden, and faded in (7.8).

**Imports:** three, `../../tokens/tokens.ts` (`realism-ambient-floor-dark`, the five `realism-star-<tint>-dark` tints through `star-layer.ts`'s `STAR_TINT_TOKENS`, `realism-belt-dark` in `belt-layer.ts`, and `realism-nebula-dark` in `nebula-layer.ts`), `../scene-model.ts` (`FILL_RENDER_ORDER`, `GLOW_RENDER_ORDER`, `BELT_RENDER_ORDER`, `NEBULA_RENDER_ORDER`, `RING_DISC_RENDER_ORDER`, `MOON_RENDER_ORDER`, and the `Body` type), `../notable-model.ts` (9.8: `RING_RADIUS_RATIO`, `MOON_RADIUS_RATIO`, `notableMoons` and `moonScale`, so the disc and the moon lie where the M2 Ring and Moon lie), `../stroke-model.ts` (`PLANET_RADIUS`, `STAR_RADIUS`, and `orbitPoints` and `ORBIT_SEGMENTS` in `belt-model.ts`, so a belt lies on its hairline) and types from `../realism-status.ts`. No Rail code: the Rail's curve arrives on the artifact as `railLine`, sampled by the mount (9.7). Within the directory, `belt-shader.ts`, `nebula-shader.ts` and `ring-shader.ts` import `glslFloat` from `star-shader.ts`, and `ring-layer.ts` the fill's `FILL_VERTEX_SHADER`. `fill-model.ts`, `star-model.ts` and `nebula-model.ts` import types only, `belt-model.ts` types and `stroke-model.ts`'s orbit, and `arrival-model.ts` imports nothing. No file here names the visitor's motion preference or `motionPace`: the mount hands the pace over as a number. No file here names `resetFrameSampling` or `chart-offer.ts`: the owner's port is the mount's (7.8). Never `src/derive/`: the artifact names the maps, so the library stays the solver's. `budgets.ts` imports nothing and re-exports nothing.
**Imported by:** `../realism-mount.tsx` alone, by one `import('./realism/index.ts')`, a test of its own directory's modules, and `spikes/realism-fills/main.tsx`, the facade alone, which ships nowhere. `scripts/` may read `budgets.ts`, and nothing else here. `scripts/bundle-assertion.test.ts` holds that, and `scripts/check-maps.test.ts` holds the budgets' reach. No module in this directory may re-export `budgets.ts`.

## The fill's rules (7.6)

- **The depth mechanism is 7.1's, exactly.** `vec4(position.xy * radius, 0, 1)` through `modelViewMatrix`, then `gl_Position.z += 1e-6 * gl_Position.w`, render order `FILL_RENDER_ORDER`, transparent pass, opacity 1, depth write and test on at rest (7.8: depth write from the frame the fade reaches 1), `FrontSide`. `1e-6` was measured only on a disc flattened to z = 0: keeping the sphere's relief would not hold at this value.
- **The disc faces the camera,** by copying its world quaternion each frame, which is what drei's `Billboard` computes. So its edge is a circle concentric with the outline, on the outline's own 64-gon.
- **One light, which never moves.** A world-space unit vector from a Planet to its own Star, from solved positions, written once at the build. Spin and tilt move only the map. There is no scene light, and no Body shadows another.
- **Spin holds on `reduced`,** handed in by the mount, and resumes from where it held. It does not use `motionPace`.
- **The tilt is about world `x`,** so a surface does not slide as the camera moves. The artifact's comment says "the Billboard's horizontal": they agree only when the camera faces along `z`.
- **Nothing is tone-mapped.** The map is `SRGBColorSpace`, the fragment includes `<colorspace_fragment>`, and `flat` stays.
- **The ceiling.** Materials normalise to `appearance.meanLuminance`. No fragment's luminance passes `stroke-active-dark`'s, a constant a test pins against the token. A saturated pixel may carry one channel above the token's. A pixel at `FILL_NEAR_CEILING` of the ceiling or above is floored to its 8-bit step, because rounding read one back above it.
- **Colour encodes nothing.** No module here or in the mount reads `mass`, `isDeep` or `moteTier`, and none but `star-model.ts` and `belt-model.ts` (9.6: an orbit's belt carries its Planet's band) reads `luminanceBand` by name (9.4: a Star's light carries its Client's recency band, AD-20 as amended; a Planet's fill still reads none). `star-layer.ts` takes the band through the plan, as `plan.band`, and never reads the field. `appearance.meanLuminance` describes a map, is read by name, and is the one brightness a fill takes from the artifact. A Star's `appearance.pulsePhase` comes from its ID and means nothing.
- **The images stay.** `dispose` frees the GPU's copies and closes no bitmap. A later Canvas builds from them. After a lost context three uploads them again on its next draw, so `uploaded` stays true. 7.8 revisited that with its explicit uploads and left it so: the warm-up is a new Canvas's, and a restore under one Canvas needs no request and no fade.
- **The seam and the pole are unmeasured on screen.** Longitude takes the smoother of two candidates, and is 0 at the exact pole, where `atan` is undefined.

`FILL_RADIUS_RATIO` is the final value of the recheck, `FILL_TARGET_LUMINANCE` is a starting value for Epic 8, and 7.9 may lower either.

## A Star's rules (7.7)

- **Two draws, on the one sphere.** `addedDraws(planets, stars, railPoints, deepPlanets)` is `planets + 2 × stars` (9.6: plus the belts' one draw when there is a Planet; 9.7: plus the nebula's one when the drawn Rail has two points or more; 9.8: plus two per Deep Planet, 24 on the committed artifact), and Dust adds none. No second geometry and no plane for a Star.
- **The fill is the Planet's mechanism, exactly:** the same vertex stage, at `FILL_RADIUS_RATIO × STAR_RADIUS`, `FILL_RENDER_ORDER`, transparent pass, opacity 1, depth write and test on at rest, following the layer's one depth switch (7.8), `FrontSide`. It has no map, reads no `appearance`, and is unlit: the token's colour taken to a target luminance, darker toward the edge.
- **The glow is additive and writes no depth,** at `GLOW_RENDER_ORDER`, with `depthTest` on and no bias, coloured by its own Star's tint alone (9.5), divided by that tint's linear luminance, so `uPeak` is the glow's absolute luminance whatever the tint; each channel is clamped to 1. Its falloff, `(1 - r²)²`, reaches 0 with no slope at its edge, so no shape is cut out. Its size is the mesh's scale, not a uniform, so three culls it by what it covers: a glow reaches past the unit sphere a fill fits inside.
- **A glow is beneath every fill, including a farther one,** so a farther fill paints over a nearer Star's glow. AD-19's draw order stands, reversing it needs a ruling, and it is measured in `realism-glow-7-7.md` for Thomas's judgement in Epic 8.
- **The subject.** A Star rises only when its own ID equals `sceneSubject`. On the Rail that is the Star the camera is heading for. In free flight to a Client, `sceneSubject` returns its `flightTarget`, so that Star rises; only the free-flight overview has no subject, and nothing rises there. In Focus the subject is a Planet, so no Star rises and white is spent once. An ID that is no Star here raises nothing. Free flight was not measured on screen.
- **The rise is one value per Star, 0 to 1,** shared by its two draws. `nextRise` steps it over `RISE_SECONDS × pace`, with the frame's delta clamped so a stall cannot cut, a delta that is not positive and finite holds it, and a pace that is not a positive finite number steps at full motion, so a falling Star always reaches rest. A layer's first frame, the hidden warm draw since 7.8, seeds it (subject 1, others 0); the arrival's fade is the layer's, not the rise's. `riseShape` eases what the shaders read.
- **The pace comes through the mount** as a number, as `focus-panel.tsx` and `corner-chrome.tsx` compute it, so reduced motion shortens the rise and never cuts it. Spin still takes no pace.
- **The ceilings.** "At rest" means settled: once every rise has arrived, nothing here passes `stroke-active-dark`'s luminance, and the subject's Star stays below `stroke-focus-dark`'s, both constants a test pins against the tokens. **A falling Star passes above the rest ceiling for a moment,** because it leaves at the subject's brightness and the AC forbids a cut: its centre read above it for 21 frames, 0.35 s, at full motion and 5 frames, 0.083 s, reduced, on the dev machine. It never passes `stroke-focus`. **During a subject change two Stars are partly white for a moment (9.4):** the core's hue goes toward white with the rise, so the falling and the rising Star are both partly white for up to `RISE_SECONDS`. The whitening is provisional: whether the subject's core turns white or keeps its tint is 9.5's open ruling (9.2's deferred finding).
- **Overlapping glows are bounded only where sampled.** Additive blending is on encoded values and a shader cannot read the destination, so a test bounds a glow's own peak, and two at rest. Three rest peaks summed on encoded values decode above the rest ceiling, so the arithmetic does not hold for three. On the committed layout, a Rail walk at 60 samples a stop, every stop and Focus found at most two glows overlapping, with no pixel over either ceiling (at rest the largest read 0.4536 against 0.47119, measured on 7.7's glows, before 9.4 changed the glow peaks by band; a re-measurement is owed in 9.11). Only those sampled poses hold this; a new layout or a pose between them may not. Pixels of the marks, at their token values, are not fill or glow pixels.
- **A Star lights nothing.** No Planet's lighting, Dust or the vignette changed, and there is no bloom, flare, composer, render target or full-screen pass. `GLOW_RADIUS_RATIO`, `GLOW_BAND_PEAK`, `GLOW_SUBJECT_PEAK` and 9.4's band ranges, dip, pulse, limb, grain and edge constants are starting values for 9.11.

## A Star's light (9.4)

Under realism a Star is its light, and its outline and core dot give way to it (AD-19, AD-20, AD-21 and AD-22 as amended).

- **Still two draws, on the one sphere:** the emissive core, which replaces 7.7's fill in the same draw, and the glow. `addedDraws` is unchanged.
- **The core is limb-darkened into its glow.** Its brightness falls from the centre to the edge by the linear law on `mu` (`STAR_CORE_LIMB` at the edge), and its alpha ramps toward 0 over the outer `STAR_CORE_EDGE` of its radius, so it melts into the additive glow beneath it (8.4's "dark ring"). **No fragment under 0.5 alpha draws or writes depth:** below `STAR_CORE_ALPHA_FLOOR` (0.5) a fragment is discarded, because once the layer writes depth a nearly invisible rim would otherwise cut later depth-tested draws (the line-work at `BODY 2`, Mote orbits, the belt and disc to come) out in a ring behind it. The soft ramp above the floor stays. `STAR_CORE_EDGE` is in `(0, 1)`, so `smoothstep` always has `edge0 < edge1`.
- **Recency is in the light.** A Star's centre at rest stays inside its band's range, `STAR_BAND_LUMINANCE`, from its Client's `luminanceBand` in the artifact. The ranges are disjoint and ordered, so a `stroke-near` Star's dimmest is above a `stroke-rest` Star's brightest at any clock, and both stay under `stroke-active`. The glow's rest peak follows the band too (`GLOW_BAND_PEAK`).
- **The pulse never leaves the band.** It is one slow sine of `STAR_PULSE_SECONDS`, phased from the Client's `appearance.pulsePhase`, moving the rest centre across the band's range.
- **The subject's centre alone reaches `stroke-focus`.** As the subject rises, the centre rises to `stroke-focus` and its hue goes to white at the same luminance: only the subject's core turns white (Proposal 7, "the subject is the one that turns white"; 9.4's provisional note resolved in 9.5), and its glow keeps its tint; each channel is then clamped to 1, which only lowers luminance, so a saturated tint (9.5) cannot clip. The subject's pulse only dims, by `STAR_SUBJECT_DIP` at most, so its peak is `stroke-focus` in linear terms at the analytic centre; on screen it approaches `stroke-focus` and never passes it, since the `FILL_NEAR_CEILING` floor lowers it by up to one 8-bit step. The limb's and the granulation's darkening is 0 at the centre alone (their factor is 1 there), so no other pixel reaches it. The ceilings hold every fragment as before, pinned against the tokens.
- **Granulation** dims only, by up to `STAR_GRAIN_DEPTH` weighted by radius, and drifts slowly with the one shared `uTime`. The clock steps by the frame's delta clamped to `RISE_MAX_FRAME_SECONDS`, so a stall cannot jump the pulse or the grain, and wraps at `STAR_CLOCK_WRAP_SECONDS` (400 pulses, an hour), so the float32 `uTime` keeps its precision. The pulse is continuous across the wrap; the granulation jumps once per wrap, which is accepted.
- **Reduced motion holds both.** The mount hands `reduced` to the layer, which hands it to the Stars' one clock (`nextClock`), as spin is. The clock holds, so the pulse and the granulation hold; the rise still steps. Nothing here names the preference.
- **The outline and the core dot fall on the one fade.** `fade()` reads the layer's one fade back. The owner's `lineWorkFade()` is `1 - fade` once the arrival has arrived, and 1 with no layer, while it warms, on the hidden warm draw, after failure, and on a fade read that throws. `body-field.tsx` reads `lineWorkFade()` in its own frame callback, which runs before the mount's because it is mounted earlier, so it reads the fade the layer reached on the previous frame, one frame late (and returns the marks one frame after the layer goes), which the spec accepts; it writes that value to each Star's billboard through `line-fade.ts`. *[9.6: the orbit hairlines now fall on the same value, with the Star marks. 9.7: and the Rail's hairline. 9.8: and a Deep Planet's Ring (held by `holdReplacedLine` under `ring:`) and Moon (`moon-field.tsx`'s pool, on the same `lineWorkFade()` read). A Planet's outline is 9.9's, the crossfade 9.10's.]*
- **A colour per Star (9.5).** Each Star's `appearance.tint` in the artifact is a short name, one of `blue`, `white`, `yellow`, `orange`, `red` (`STAR_TINT_NAMES`, pinned equal to derive's `STAR_TINTS`), checked by `starPlans`, which throws naming the Client on one that is unknown or missing. `star-layer.ts` alone turns it into a token, `realism-star-<tint>-dark`, through its explicit record `STAR_TINT_TOKENS`, and the Star's core and glow share that one `Color`; no other material reads a tint, and it never changes in time. **The tint is hue, never brightness:** the core and the glow each divide it by its own linear luminance, so at the same band, pulse and rise every tint's centre and glow peak have the same luminance. **At rest no channel clamps,** for any tint, band or pulse, and none does on the risen subject (its hue is white), so this is exact there. Only mid-rise, while a subject rises or falls (at most `RISE_SECONDS`), can a saturated tint clamp a channel, which only lowers luminance, by at most `WORST_CLAMP_LOSS` (0.24%, measured over the tint × band × pulse × rise grid and pinned in `star-model.test.ts`): a transient, never a resting difference. The glow peaks are absolute luminance, 9.4's shares (0.16, 0.22, 0.6) × the linear luminance of `#B09468` (`GLOW_94_LUMINANCE`), so every glow is exactly as bright as in 9.4. Every ceiling, band range and glow bound is tested per tint. The tints, band ranges, dip, pulse period, limb, grain and edge are starting values for 9.11.

## The belt (9.6)

Under realism every orbit is a faint debris belt that carries its Planet's recency, and its hairline gives way to it (AD-19 and AD-20 as amended).

- **One draw for every belt.** One `BufferGeometry`, one `ShaderMaterial` and one `Mesh`, named `belt`, built by `createBelt` into the layer's one group: `addedDraws(planets, stars, 0)` is `planets + 2 × stars + 1` when there is a Planet, and `2 × stars` when there is none, `BELT_DRAWS_BUDGET` being 1. No `Points`, sprite or instancing.
- **On the hairline's own orbit.** `beltPlans` builds each Planet's orbit with `stroke-model.ts`'s `orbitPoints(planet, star, ORBIT_SEGMENTS)`, so a belt is centred where the hairline is, in the solved `orbitNormal` plane. `beltArrays` widens it into an indexed triangle strip from `1 - w` to `1 + w` of the radius about its Star, `w` being `BELT_HALF_WIDTH_RATIO` (0.08). Each vertex carries `aBelt` (along the orbit in `[0, 1]`, across the band in `[-1, 1]`) and `aLuminance`, its band's.
- **Recency is in the brightness.** A band's peak linear luminance is its stroke token's own, `BELT_BAND_LUMINANCE` (`stroke-rest-dark` about 0.026, `stroke-near-dark` about 0.061), restated and pinned against the tokens. So a belt's peak is its Planet's own band token's luminance (a `stroke-rest` belt's is the M2 orbit's level; the M2 orbit was always `stroke-rest`, so a `stroke-near` belt peaks brighter than the hairline it replaces), the order is the ladder's by construction, and every belt is far under `stroke-active`. The colour is `realism-belt-dark` divided by its own linear luminance, times the band's, each channel clamped to 1 (`beltColor`, the shader's line in JS): one hue for every belt, encoding nothing. Normal blending only moves a pixel toward that colour, whose channels are all under 0.07, so over the void, a `stroke-active` grey or any tint at its rest peak no belt pixel lifts a destination above the ceiling; `belt-model.test.ts` checks that grid.
- **Not Dust.** The alpha is `(1 - t²)²` across the band, times `mix(BELT_DENSITY_FLOOR, 1, n)` for a static value noise `n` in `[0, 1]`, times the one `uFade`. The noise is continuous, with no threshold, `step` or discard, so there are no specks, and its cells along the orbit wrap modulo `BELT_NOISE_CELLS.along`, so the belt is seamless where its orbit closes. There is no time uniform, and the layer's `frame` never touches the mesh, so the belt is identical frame to frame and nothing changes with `reduced`.
- **Its draw.** `BELT_RENDER_ORDER` (1.6), after the fills and depth-tested against them, so its near side passes over its Planet as an M2 orbit's near half does, and before the line-work at `BODY 2`. Normal blending, `transparent`, `depthWrite` false at every fade, `depthTest` true, `DoubleSide` with `forceSinglePass` (three draws a transparent `DoubleSide` material twice, back faces then front, unless it is set, which would make the one merged mesh two GL draws), `<colorspace_fragment>`. The shader's normalisation is guarded at `max(dot(uColor, WEIGHTS), 1e-6)`, and `createFillLayer` reads `beltTint()`, which refuses a token with no luminance through `beltColor`, before it builds anything. **During the fade the fills write no depth,** so a belt's far side shows through a Planet's fill until the fade reaches 1; that is 9.10's (the deferred 9.2 finding).
- **The arrival is the layer's.** The belt reads the one fade, is compiled and link-checked by `warm` with every other material, and is freed by `dispose`. The orbit hairlines (`orbit:*` from `sceneLines`) are held by `line-fade.ts`'s `holdOrbitLine` (renamed `holdReplacedLine` by 9.7) and fall on `lineWorkFade()` in `body-field.tsx`'s one frame loop, whole and together with the Star marks, and return at their token values if realism fails. *[9.7: the Rail's line (`rail`) now falls with them.]*
- `BELT_HALF_WIDTH_RATIO`, `BELT_DENSITY_FLOOR`, `BELT_NOISE_CELLS` and `realism-belt` are starting values for 9.11.

## The nebula (9.7)

Under realism the Rail is a faint, noise-shaded nebula ribbon, and its hairline gives way to it (AD-19, AD-20 and AD-23 as amended). The ribbon is not a mark and carries no data.

- **One draw, on exactly the drawn curve.** `realism-mount.tsx` samples `railLine(rail, RAIL_LINE_SAMPLES_PER_SEGMENT)`, the curve `body-field.tsx` draws the hairline on, memoised on the artifact and the Rail, and hands the owner `{ ...artifact, railLine }`; `RealismArtifact.railLine` is not part of the committed artifact, and nothing here imports Rail code. `nebulaArrays` turns it into one indexed strip, two vertices per point, both at the point, each with `aRibbon` (arc length in scene units, across `∓1`) and the central-difference `aTangent` (`Uint32Array` indices past 65,535 vertices). `createNebula` builds one `BufferGeometry`, one `ShaderMaterial` and one `Mesh`, named `nebula`: `NEBULA_DRAWS_BUDGET` is 1, and `addedDraws` counts it when the curve has two points or more (22 on the committed artifact: 8 + 12 + 1 + 1). Under two points there is no ribbon and no draw, as there is no hairline.
- **The width faces the camera.** In view space the vertex stage moves each vertex `across × NEBULA_HALF_WIDTH` (3, a Star's glow reach) along `normalize(cross(t, toCamera))`; where that is degenerate (`|side|² ≤ 1e-6·|t|²·|toCamera|²`) it falls back to `cross(t, viewUp)`, then to view `+x`, so it is never NaN, even with the camera riding along the Rail. `ribbonSide` is that line in JS. The bounding sphere is the centreline's grown by the half-width, so three never culls a ribbon on screen. The mesh never moves (`matrixAutoUpdate` off).
- **The brightness is a ceiling.** The colour is `realism-nebula-dark` divided by its own linear luminance, times `NEBULA_PEAK_LUMINANCE` (0.0076), each channel clamped to 1 (`nebulaColor`, guarded in the shader at `max(dot(uColor, WEIGHTS), 1e-6)`; `nebulaTint()` is read before anything is built). Its alpha is `(1 - t²)²` across, times a `smoothstep` over `NEBULA_END_UNITS` (8) at each end, times `smoothstep(NEBULA_DENSITY_LOW, NEBULA_DENSITY_HIGH, fbm)`, times the one `uFade`, with no `step` or discard, so there are no specks and it cannot read as Dust. The output is `vec4(colour × alpha, 1.0)`, then `<colorspace_fragment>`, blended additively as the glow is. Additive blending adds encoded values, so `nebula-model.test.ts` pins its bounds over what it adds to: over `surface-base-dark` it decodes to at most `stroke-dust-dark`'s luminance (about 0.01252 against 0.01255), the Rail line it replaces; a Mote label (`ink-label-dark`, no plate) over it reads about 4.9:1 against its 4.5 floor; and for every ordered pair of tints, the void, the peak and two near glow peaks stay under `stroke-active` (about 0.447 at worst), and with the subject's glow and a near one under `stroke-focus` (about 0.742 at worst). **These bounds are for one layer of ribbon at its peak.** The ribbon is additive, `DoubleSide` and writes no depth, so where it overlaps itself on screen (looking along the Rail, or across a tight bend) its light adds once per layer and can pass every bound above. Nothing limits that in code; it is a check for 9.11's rendered-pixel pass.
- **The drift.** The fbm is periodic in x with period `NEBULA_NOISE_PERIOD` (64 cells): each octave's hash wraps x modulo its period, the octaves double the frequency and the period, and shift on y only (`nebulaFbm` is its JS mirror, pinned equal at `x` and `x + period`). `uDrift`, in cells, steps by `nextNebulaDrift(drift, delta, reduced)`: held while `reduced` is set or on a delta that is not finite and above 0, otherwise `(drift + delta × NEBULA_DRIFT) mod NEBULA_NOISE_PERIOD`, so the wrap lands on the identical field. The hash is sine-free (Dave Hoskins' `hash12`, mirrored exactly in `nebulaFbm`): it takes the wrapped cell through `fract(p × 0.1031)` first, so its intermediates stay bounded (below about 2.2e4; a bound on range, so it still needs highp's precision) at every cell, octave and drift, where a sine hash would see arguments near 1e5 at the fourth octave. The layer's `frame` steps it on `reduced`, as the spin is, never on the pace; nothing else in the mesh changes after it is built.
- **Its draw.** `NEBULA_RENDER_ORDER` (1.1), after the Dust and before every glow, fill, core and plate, so each paints over it and it never hazes a Planet (AD-19, 2026-11-18). `AdditiveBlending`, `transparent`, `depthWrite` false, `depthTest` true, `DoubleSide` with `forceSinglePass`. During the fade the fills are below opacity 1, so the ribbon shows through them; that is 9.10's (the deferred 9.2 finding).
- **The arrival is the layer's.** The ribbon reads the one fade, is compiled and link-checked by `warm` with every other material, and is freed by `dispose`. The Rail's hairline (`rail`) is held by `line-fade.ts`'s `holdReplacedLine` with the orbits' and falls on `lineWorkFade()`, whole, with the Star marks and the orbits; it returns at its token value if realism fails or never arrives.
- `NEBULA_HALF_WIDTH`, `NEBULA_PEAK_LUMINANCE`, `NEBULA_END_UNITS`, the density bounds, `NEBULA_NOISE_SCALE`, `NEBULA_DRIFT` and `realism-nebula` are starting values for 9.11. The peak is not, in practice: at the pinned 0.0076, void plus peak is about 0.01252 against the 0.01255 bound, so there is essentially no headroom at the void bound, and raising it by any useful amount needs a ruling that loosens that bound.

## The ring disc and moon sphere (9.8)

Under realism a Deep Planet's Ring hairline and Moon dot give way to a thin, tilted, semi-transparent ring disc and a small lit moon sphere (DESIGN.md §7.2, AD-19 and AD-23 as amended). Both read `isDeep` alone, through `ringPlans`: no band, mass, subject, `appearance` or `content`, so every Deep Planet's disc and moon are the same apart from position and light direction, and the signal uses neither size nor brightness (FR-9).

- **Where they lie: on the M2 marks.** The disc's outer edge is `RING_RADIUS_RATIO × PLANET_RADIUS`, the Ring's; the moon's centre is the M2 Moon's billboard-local `center` and its radius the M2 Moon's, both from `notableMoons`. So the Client-name hide rule and the leader's `markPx`, which read those marks, stay correct unchanged.
- **The disc.** One `RingGeometry` from `RING_DISC_INNER` (1.35 body radii) to `RING_DISC_OUTER`, shared by every disc. Each frame it takes the camera's facing turned by `RING_DISC_TILT` (0.7 rad) about its local x, so it is one constant ellipse from every pose and never edge-on. At that tilt its inner minor half-axis, `cos(0.7) × 0.6075 ≈ 0.465`, lies beyond the fill's 0.448, so its own fill never crosses it (the spike's 1.15 rad did). Its colour is grey, `vec3(RING_DISC_LUMINANCE)`, `stroke-near-dark`'s linear luminance restated and pinned, the Ring's rung; its alpha is `edge × bands × RING_DISC_OPACITY × uFade`, `edge` a `smoothstep` at both radial edges, `bands` a static radial value noise over the nebula's sine-free hash in `[RING_DISC_BAND_FLOOR, 1]`, `RING_DISC_BAND_CELLS` (6) cells from inner to outer edge, so the bands stay coarser than the few pixels the band spans at a Client stop and do not shimmer as the camera moves, with no `step` and no discard. Over the void (`surface-base-dark`) the disc decodes to about 0.026 linear luminance at its peak alpha with the bands at 1, and about 0.012 at the band floor. The radius is computed per fragment from the disc coordinate. `NormalBlending`, `transparent`, depth-tested, no depth write, `DoubleSide` with `forceSinglePass`, at `RING_DISC_RENDER_ORDER` (1.75): over the belts, under the line-work at `BODY 2`.
- **The moon.** One mesh on the layer's shared sphere, on `FILL_VERTEX_SHADER` (flattened in the marks' plane, as a fill is) with its own fragment stage: Lambert from its Planet's `lightDirection`, `y = MOON_ALBEDO_LUMINANCE × max(N·L, 0) + MOON_FLOOR_LUMINANCE`, output `vec4(vec3(y), uFade)`. `transparent`, depth-tested, no depth write, `FrontSide`, at `MOON_RENDER_ORDER`, so it is always over its disc. Each frame its position is `planet + facing · center`, its quaternion the facing, and its scale `moonScale(plan, view, projection, heightPx)`, `heightPx` read from the renderer `warm` was handed (`getSize`); before `warm` there is no renderer, and the scale is 1, as it is at height 0 or with the Planet behind the camera.
- **Brightness.** Normal blending never passes its brighter input, so the disc (at most `stroke-near`) and the moon (at most 0.18 + the floor, a Planet's lit mean) stay under `stroke-active`, grey by construction; `ring-model.test.ts` pins both.
- **Two draws per Deep Planet**, `DEEP_PROJECT_DRAWS_BUDGET` (2): the disc is single-pass, so the moon's share is 1 and no ruling is needed. `addedDraws` is 24 on the committed artifact (8 + 12 + 1 + 1 + 2), the layer's meshes the same.
- **Readable at a Client stop.** `signal.test.ts` measures, on the committed layout at every integer Client stop at 1920 × 1080 with the Deep Planet in frame, the moon at least `MOON_MIN_RADIUS_PX`, the disc's projected outer minor half-axis at least `RING_DISC_MIN_PX` (3) and its visible band along the minor axis (outer edge minus inner edge) at least `RING_DISC_MIN_BAND_PX` (2). The least minor half-axis is about 7.3 px and the least band about 2.83 px, both at the neighbouring Client's stop; at the Planet's own stop the half-axis is about 38.5 px. A control re-measures with the disc nearly edge-on (1.53 rad) and fails both floors. **At the opening frame** (stop 0, the whole sky, not a Client stop) the disc reads about 1.1 px, against about 1.4 px for the M2 Ring it replaces: realism lowers Deep/Shallow legibility at the first view. That is measured and pinned, not held to a floor; it is 9.11's to judge on screen.
- **The arrival is the layer's.** Both materials read the one fade, are compiled and link-checked by `warm` and freed by `dispose`. The Ring line falls on `lineWorkFade()` through `holdReplacedLine` (`ring:`), and the Moon dot through `moon-field.tsx`'s one frame loop (`fadeStarMarks` skips a handle not mounted yet), whole with the Star marks, orbits and Rail; both return at their token values if realism fails. A Planet's outline stays at its token value (9.9).
- **Two artefacts during the fade, 9.10's (the crossfade).** While the fade runs the fills write no depth, so a far disc or moon shows over a nearer fill, as the belt does. And the M2 Moon dot and the moon sphere share `MOON_RENDER_ORDER` at the same place, so three's depth sort can flicker between them until the dot has faded out.
- `RING_DISC_INNER_RATIO`, `RING_DISC_TILT`, `RING_DISC_OPACITY`, `RING_DISC_BAND_FLOOR`, `RING_DISC_BAND_CELLS`, `RING_DISC_EDGE`, `RING_DISC_SEGMENTS`, `RING_DISC_MIN_PX` and `RING_DISC_MIN_BAND_PX` are starting values for 9.11.

## The arrival's rules (7.8)

- **Warm before anything is seen.** `warm(renderer, camera, scene)` calls `initTexture` once per texture, then `compileAsync(group, camera, scene)` once, over the layer's own `Group` while it is in no scene, so R3F's next render cannot link the programs synchronously first, and against the scene it will join, so the programs are the ones that scene's draw looks up. A `Group` is not a draw. Then it reads each material's program `LINK_STATUS` straight from the context, which logs nothing, and rejects if any is not linked: three's own check runs on first use, and logs. `warm` attaches nothing.
- **The hidden warm draw.** The owner attaches the group at fade 0 on the frame after the warm-up resolves. At `uFade = 0` a fill blends to the destination unchanged under `NormalBlending` and a glow adds 0 under `AdditiveBlending`, so that frame binds every texture and program for real and shows nothing, with no render target or extra pass.
- **One fade.** One `{ value }` object, created in `fill-layer.ts` and shared by every Planet fill, Star fill and glow. A fill's alpha is `uFade`; a glow multiplies its colour by it. `arrive(true)` starts it stepping with each frame by `nextFade`, over `FADE_SECONDS × pace` with the delta clamped to `FADE_MAX_FRAME_SECONDS`, a bad delta holding it and a bad pace stepping at full motion; `arrive(false)` sets it to 1 at once. Fading only mixes a pixel toward what is already drawn, so the ceilings hold at every fade value.
- **Depth waits for 1.** Every fill is built with `depthWrite` false and switches on together on the frame the fade reaches 1, by `depthWrites`; with no fade, on the first visible frame. So while the layer fades, no line-work behind a fill vanishes, and once it has arrived a farther Body's marks behind a fill are hidden as in 7.7. A glow never writes depth.
- `FADE_SECONDS` (1.0) is a starting value, for Epic 8. The browser record is `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/realism-arrival-7-8.md`.

## The signal and the labels (7.9)

- **No fill hides a Ring or a Moon at a stop.** `signal.test.ts` projects, over the committed artifact at `DESKTOP_ASPECT` into 1920×1080, every Ring (64 samples on its circle) and every Moon (its centre and 16 rim points at its pixel-floored radius) at each integer Rail stop 0..N, with the camera `scene.tsx` builds, and fails naming the stop, the Body and the mark when a sample lies inside a nearer Body's fill disc, `FILL_RADIUS_RATIO × bodyRadius`, Planet and Star alike. Every fill, Ring and Moon is a disc parallel to the image plane, so each projects to a circle and the check is 2D, with the Body's view depth for "nearer". A Body's own fill never counts, glows write no depth and are never occluders, and points off screen or behind the camera are skipped. It fails too when no Ring and no Moon were tested. **It reads integer stops only:** hiding between stops, in transit, is accepted and not checked (AR-M3-16). It holds at rest, when fills write depth. Two controls show it can fail: a Body moved between the camera and the Ring, as a re-solve might, and an artifact with its Deep marks stripped.
- **The ceiling does not protect a label.** `ink-label-dark` is about 1.7:1 on `stroke-active-dark` and 5.85:1 on `surface-base-dark`; it keeps 4.5:1 only on a ground of relative luminance up to about 0.018, against `FILL_TARGET_LUMINANCE` 0.18. `signal.test.ts` states that from the tokens, through `src/shell/contrast.ts`.
- **Labels are measured on real pixels,** by `spikes/realism-fills/prod-labels.mjs`, against a `chunk-404` build at the same pose. The record is `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/realism-labels-7-9.md`. Over a lit Star the affordance's two lines fell to about 1:1, and only Stars put out at the ground could clear that from here. So, by Thomas's ruling, the affordance stands on a plate of `surface-base` (`affordance-model.ts`). The realism values are unchanged.
