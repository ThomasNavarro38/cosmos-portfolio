# PRD Quality Review — Cosmos Portfolio

## Overall verdict

This is a strong PRD, and unusually so in the dimensions that are normally weakest: it has a real thesis (top-of-funnel, forwardability, "reasoning is visible"), it names what it gave up rather than smoothing choices to neutral, and its Non-Goals and Rejected Alternatives sections do genuine work instead of decorating. The FR set is mostly written as testable consequences rather than adjectives, which is exactly what `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` will need.

What is at risk is concentrated and identifiable. Three FRs — FR-9, FR-20, FR-22 — carry the launch gate but resolve to "we will decide this later," and FR-20 (Schematic Identity) is the one the PRD itself flags as the widest-variance launch task while giving it the least specification of any launch FR. The open-items density (12 Open Questions, 6 unratified assumptions, 7 `[NOTE FOR PM]`) is high for a document the author calls launch-stakes, and while most items are honestly deferred to M1 where they are cheapest, at least four of them gate M2 and are not scheduled anywhere. There is also one concrete data-model inconsistency (duration vs `built`) that will bite architecture.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Trade-offs are stated as decisions with the loss named, which is rare. FR-8 Out of Scope does not merely drop measured outcomes, it explains why the substitute works: "anyone can claim a performance improvement; nobody invents 'no container runtime on prem.'" NFR-2 does not just pick 30fps, it argues why 30 rather than 60 for this specific content ("slow camera movement through mostly static geometry, the exact case where 30 reads cinematic rather than broken") and names what holding 60 would cost. §6 splits tradeable from not-tradeable explicitly and then admits the previous framing was the problem: the not-tradeables "had been functioning as preconditions to existing, which is what kept the finish line receding."

Objections are met rather than dodged. The obvious pushback on this product — "backward time is user-hostile" — is answered in §2.2 by scoping who the reader is ("that information is upstream in the CV and LinkedIn profile"). The second obvious pushback — "a greybox launch looks unfinished" — is answered in §4.5 and then again in §12 Launch framing, where the honest-beta alternative is rejected with a reason ("invites the exact judgment it tries to pre-empt"). §7 pre-emptively refuses a competitive claim and states *why the absence is a decision*: "judged unnecessary rather than skipped."

The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions, not safe checkpoints — FR-9's is at the most likely gate failure, FR-18's is at the pacing numbers nobody has, FR-19's is at an admitted hole. Open Questions are genuinely open: Q3 ("A committed date, a named first reader, or both? If neither, R-1 is accepted as a named risk rather than mitigated") states the consequence of not deciding, which is the mark of a real question rather than a rhetorical one.

### Findings

- **medium** Q3 is the load-bearing question and has no forcing function of its own (§13 Q3, R-1) — R-1 is rated **Critical** and its mitigation is an `[ASSUMPTION]` that has not been ratified. The PRD is honest that "if neither, R-1 is accepted as a named risk," but nothing in the document schedules the decision, and the only stated deadline mechanism *is* the thing under question. Every other open question can be tuned at M1; this one cannot be tuned at all. *Fix:* Give Q3 a resolution point tied to a milestone — e.g. "resolved before M1 starts" — so the capacity risk is not the only item without a gate.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona theater: there are four named protagonists (Marc, Priya, Tom, Thomas) and each drives specific requirements. Marc drives NFR-1 and FR-22; Priya drives FR-8, FR-9, FR-13, FR-18; Tom drives FR-24 and the entire funnel-positioning frame; Thomas drives FR-1..FR-6 and NFR-5. Remove any one and requirements go unmotivated — that is the test, and it passes.

No NFR theater. NFR-4 names a specific machine ("a three-year-old laptop with integrated graphics at 1080p") and then names what it is *not* ("explicitly not a current Apple-silicon machine and not the dev machine"), which is the sentence that makes the NFR enforceable. NFR-3 is a genuine policy with a rationale, not a platitude: "Better the real thing at 24fps than a lesser thing at 30."

The Vision (§1) could not swap into another PRD — it names the mechanism ("positions are solved by a force simulation from a single embedded JSON file"), and §1's closing paragraph does something most PRDs will not do: it states the real motive ("The author wanted to build it") and refuses to launder it, then §6 makes non-sanitisation a binding editorial constraint on downstream documents.

### Findings

- **low** FR-16's second consequence is not a consequence (§4.4 FR-16) — "Backward time serves three masters at once: an aesthetic conceit, the correct ordering for a professional reader, and a non-arbitrary home for the ending. One decision, three payoffs" is rationale sitting in a **Consequences (testable)** list, where nothing about it is testable. It is good rationale; it is in the wrong slot, and a story-generation pass reading the consequence lists mechanically will emit a non-story from it. *Fix:* Move to the FR body or a Notes line, leaving the single genuine consequence ("the most recent entry is encountered first").

## Strategic coherence — strong

There is a thesis and it is stated in one sentence: "it does not change a hiring decision, it changes whether the conversation happens at all, and whether the link gets forwarded. It is top of funnel." Everything downstream follows from it. Because the bet is forwardability, FR-24 (shareability) exists and the PRD flags it as new — "it appears nowhere in the brief and was introduced by funnel positioning." Because the bet is The Forward who has no context, NFR-1 (First Paint) is carved out of the tradeable list. Because success is a conversation rather than a hire, §7 explicitly renounces the broader claim.

Prioritisation follows the thesis rather than ease. FR-20 (Schematic Identity) is promoted to launch blocker precisely because it is hard and load-bearing, and R-4 says so: "sits in the author's weakest discipline and now gates launch." The realism pass — the fun part, the part a builder would naturally do first — is pushed to M3 with an explicit statement of why that inverts the dynamic ("No longer a launch gate; an upgrade to a live site").

Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity, and each names what it validates. SM-1 measures the forward itself. The counter-metrics are the strongest part of §11 and are correctly chosen as the exact metrics a drifting version of this product would start optimising: SM-C2 (time on site) "would reward padding entries — the precise failure two Project classes were adopted to avoid"; SM-C4 (visual fidelity before launch) "restores the receding finish line that Flag 03 removed." Naming SM-C4 as a counter-metric is the PRD arguing against its own author's likely behaviour, which is the point of the section.

MVP scope kind is coherent — an *experience* MVP with a single gate question (§8.1: "Can a stranger tell what Thomas does — and can he tell which entries are worth reading?") that both SM-2 and the M2 milestone row restate identically.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs carry consequences an engineer can write a test or a check against, and several are unusually crisp because they are stated as prohibitions, which are easier to verify than capabilities: FR-2 "No Body position appears as a literal coordinate in any source or data file"; FR-17 "No Shot definition contains a world coordinate"; FR-10 "No technology name appears in any `notable`"; FR-13 "No Skill body exists in the scene while its Project is unfocused." FR-4's "An entry omitting `weight` is treated as `1`" is a unit test as written. FR-8's "below roughly 90 words is rejected at authoring time" is an enforceable authoring gate. NFR-1 and NFR-2 both carry numbers and a named hardware profile.

The weakness is concentrated in the visual and fallback FRs, which is also where the PRD's own risk register says the launch is most likely to fail — so the thinness is on the FRs that can least afford it.

### Findings

- **high** FR-20 has no definition of done, on the FR the PRD calls its highest-variance launch task (§4.5 FR-20, R-4) — the consequences are "A defined palette, line weight, typography, label treatment, and UI chrome exist and are applied consistently" and "No element reads as a placeholder or as an untextured asset awaiting a pass." The first is satisfied by the existence of *any* five artifacts; the second is an unbounded aesthetic judgment with no judge named. R-4's mitigation says to give it "its own definition of done" — the PRD asks for that DoD without supplying it, so nothing in the launch set gates on it. This is the single biggest done-ness gap because FR-20 is a launch blocker whose failure mode is invisible to tests. *Fix:* Either state who renders the verdict and when (the same named first reader from R-1 is the obvious candidate, answering a second question cold), or push the DoD explicitly to `bmad-ux` as a required deliverable so the gap is owned rather than open.

- **high** FR-9's distinguishability has no threshold and its only specification is an unratified assumption (§4.2 FR-9) — "A visitor can tell which entries carry a Notable before committing to reading one" and "A Deep Project is visually distinguishable from a Shallow Project in the scene." Distinguishable to whom, and verified how? The actual visual treatment lives entirely inside `[ASSUMPTION: A Shallow Project renders dimmer, carries no condensed-skill "moon" at rest…]`, which §14 states is "not a decision the user has ratified" and §13 Q5 leaves open. FR-9 is named in the M2 gate ("can he tell which entries are worth reading") and in SM-2, so a launch-gating requirement is currently specified only by a proposal. *Fix:* The addendum already identifies the cheap resolution and says it "has no dependencies" — run the two-entries-cold-read, ratify or amend the assumption, then restate FR-9's consequence against the chosen treatment.

- **medium** FR-22 defers both its trigger and its content (§4.7 FR-22) — "Detection occurs before the visitor sees a failed or degraded 3D scene" specifies ordering but not the detection mechanism, and "conveys what the site is and what Thomas does" restates the M2 gate without bounding it. The Notes admit this ("what detects an incapable machine, and how much the flat chart must convey"), and the addendum sharpens it: "'Flat star chart' names a form, not a content set." Given §2.2 puts *all* mobile visitors and all non-WebGL corporate laptops on the Fallback, this is not an edge path — it is plausibly the majority path for UJ-3, whose entire climax depends on it. *Fix:* At minimum state the detection signal (WebGL context creation + viewport/pointer check) and hold the Fallback to the same M2 gate question explicitly, so it inherits a testable bar rather than an adjective.

- **medium** FR-23's only consequence is existence (§4.7 FR-23) — "A visitor can initiate contact from the launched site" plus "explicitly unglamorous." §13 Q10 notes "a mailto is the zero-infrastructure option" and the addendum flags that third-party form services "would violate NFR-6's spirit if not its letter." The decision is one line and the PRD has already reasoned it through in two other documents; leaving it open buys nothing and leaves a launch-scope item unspecified. *Fix:* Ratify mailto in FR-23 and close Q10, or state the constraint that would rule it out.

- **low** FR-15's second consequence is an adjective pair (§4.3 FR-15) — "Dust density does not obscure Bodies or read as fog." "Reads as fog" is precisely the judgment call the Notes defer to M1 tuning, which is the right call; the issue is only that it sits in a **testable** list. *Fix:* Mark it as an M1 tuning target rather than a consequence, consistent with how the Notes already treat it.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and reasoned, and the PRD distinguishes categories of omission that most documents collapse. §8.2 separates *deferred* (realism → M3, collapse → M4) from *permanently rejected* ("Per-entry position overrides — permanently rejected, not deferred"; "CMS or backend of any kind — permanently rejected"), and from *revisitable-on-trigger* ("Belt-merging… Revisit only on several-fold growth"). §12 exists specifically so rejected options are not re-litigated, and it records *why* each was rejected in a clause, not just that it was.

Several FRs carry their own Out of Scope blocks that pre-empt silent assumption — FR-11's exclusion of client relationship friction is recorded explicitly "so the decision is not silently revisited," and the addendum repeats it under **Deliberately Excluded from the PRD**. De-scoping is proposed rather than performed: §14 opens with "All six are proposals filling gaps the upstream chain left explicitly open — none is a decision the user has ratified," which is the honest framing.

Open-items density is the one soft spot. 12 Open Questions + 6 assumptions + 7 `[NOTE FOR PM]` = 25 open items for a document described as launch-stakes. Most are correctly parked (Q6, Q7 → "Tune at M1," which is where they are cheapest, and the PRD says so in §9). But Q5, Q9, Q10, Q11 and Q12 all touch M2 scope, and §8.1 lists FR-9, FR-21, FR-22, FR-23 as frozen launch scope while their specifications remain proposals. The PRD is not hiding this — it is a real tension it has named rather than resolved.

### Findings

- **medium** Four launch-scope FRs are "frozen" in §8.1 while their content is still an open question (§8.1 vs §13 Q5/Q9/Q10/Q11) — §6's scope guardrail says "M2 is frozen as defined in §8.1," but FR-9, FR-21, FR-22 and FR-23 are frozen only by title. A guardrail that freezes a list of headings does not prevent the scope growth R-5 warns about, because the growth would happen inside FR-9 and FR-22 when they are finally specified. *Fix:* Note in §8.1 which frozen items are title-frozen but content-open, and apply the "actively misleading without it" bar when they are resolved — not just to new additions.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This is a chain-top PRD (§0 names UX, architecture, and epic/story breakdown as consumers), so this dimension carries real weight. The scaffolding is largely right: the Glossary (§3) is genuine — every term is defined with a cardinality or a rule, not just a synonym, and several definitions carry a prohibition that downstream must honour ("**Never a body**"; "**Never world coordinates.**"). Terms are used verbatim across FRs, UJs and SM definitions with only trivial drift. FR/UJ/SM IDs are contiguous and unique (FR-1..FR-24, UJ-1..UJ-4, SM-1..SM-5 + SM-C1..SM-C4, NFR-1..NFR-7, R-1..R-7), and every cross-reference I checked resolves. The addendum's routing table is a genuinely useful downstream artifact — it tells a reader which of four upstream documents is authoritative per question, and restates the conflict rule.

Sections mostly stand alone. FRs reference by ID and Glossary term rather than "see above," and the "Realizes UJ-n" tags give story generation a traceability path in both directions. The addendum's per-workflow sections (`bmad-architecture`, `bmad-ux`, inventory pass) are pre-sorted for exactly the consumers §0 names, and the UX section is candid about a gap the PRD itself does not surface.

The problem is one data-model inconsistency that will surface as a question the moment architecture reads FR-3 against FR-7.

### Findings

- **high** The mapping rules consume a `duration` that the schema does not contain (FR-3, FR-5, FR-7, §8.3) — FR-3 states "mass from duration," FR-5 keys on "no parent and no duration," and the FR-21 assumption annotates "mass from this duration." But FR-7's fixed field set is "client, project, company, role, built, team_size, notable, skills" — there is no `duration` field, and `built` is undefined in the Glossary and appears in no other requirement. Either `built` is a date range from which duration is derived, or duration is a ninth field missing from FR-7. Downstream this is not cosmetic: FR-7's consequence "No Project has a field another Project lacks" makes the schema a contract, and both `bmad-architecture` and story generation will read FR-7 as the authoritative field list. *Fix:* Define `built` in the Glossary and state whether duration is derived from it or is a separate field; if derived, say so in FR-3 so the mapping rule names a real input.

- **medium** `weight` is absent from FR-7's schema while being required of every entry (FR-4, FR-7) — FR-4 says "Each entry carries one manual relevance value" and NFR-5/SM-4 make "one JSON entry plus one `weight` value" the maintenance contract, but `weight` does not appear in FR-7's identical field set. FR-4's default rule ("An entry omitting `weight` is treated as `1`") suggests it is optional-with-default rather than absent, but FR-7 reads as exhaustive. *Fix:* Either add `weight` to FR-7's list marked optional-defaulting-to-1, or state that FR-7 covers content fields and layout fields are separate.

- **low** FR-3's third rule is stated in two vocabularies (FR-3 vs FR-21 assumption) — FR-3 says "distance from the Rail by relevance"; the FR-21 assumption says "distance from this weight." Both are correct if relevance *is* `weight`, but the Glossary defines Weight as "the single manual relevance knob," and FR-3 is the only place the abstract term is used. Since the Colophon must show "the three mapping rules… visibly tied to actual values," the rule should name the field. *Fix:* State FR-3's third rule as "distance from the Rail by `weight`."

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product on every axis the rubric names. This is a consumer-facing artifact with meaningful UX and a genuinely multi-audience read (screener, peer, cold forward), so UJs are load-bearing rather than overhead — and all four have named protagonists carrying context inline (Marc "has the PDF open in one tab and eleven others behind it"; Tom "gets a Slack message… containing only a URL and the word 'look'"). Each UJ has a climax and a resolution, and three carry edge cases that map to actual FRs (UJ-1 and UJ-3 edge cases → FR-22; UJ-4 edge case → FR-5's Shallow-Project consequence).

UJ-4 deserves specific credit for shape: making the *builder* a protagonist is the right call for a product whose central architectural claim is maintainability, and it converts NFR-5 and SM-4 from assertions into a walkthrough. It is the journey that makes FR-1..FR-6 legible as a feature rather than an implementation note.

The hobby/solo shape is respected without dropping the substance bar — commercial framing (unit economics, pricing, stakeholder signoff, headcount) is absent, correctly, and the PRD substitutes what actually constrains this project: capacity (R-1), scope discipline (R-5, §6), and skill gaps (R-4, R-6). Four UJs and 24 FRs is proportionate; this is not an over-formalised document.

Chain-top status is handled well — §0 states who consumes it, the addendum sorts depth per consumer, and §7's `[NOTE FOR PM]` binds downstream documents ("If any downstream document asserts otherwise, it must research it first"), which is the correct place to put a constraint that outlives this document.

### Findings

- **medium** The information layer has no specified form anywhere in the PRD (§4.2, NFR-7, addendum *Visual System*) — FR-7 and FR-8 specify its content and NFR-7 makes legibility "a hard requirement, equal in weight to awe," but nothing states whether 150 words of prose appears as an overlay, a panel, or in-scene text. It is named in FR-6 ("Company is displayed as a label or tint on the Project's information layer"), FR-9, FR-13 and the M2 milestone title, so it is a first-class launch object referenced by four FRs and defined by none — it does not even appear in the Glossary, unlike Colophon and Fallback. The addendum catches this ("This is a genuine gap and belongs squarely to UX"), so the gap is known; it is unowned only in the PRD. *Fix:* Add **Information layer** to the Glossary defining it as the non-3D surface carrying a Focused Project's fields and prose, and state in §4.2 that its presentation is delegated to `bmad-ux` — turning an omission into an assignment.

## Mechanical notes

- **Assumptions Index roundtrips cleanly.** All six inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (FR-9, FR-21, NFR-1, NFR-2, R-1, R-6) appear in §14, and every §14 entry has an inline source with a matching section reference. Each is also paired to an Open Question as §0 claims (Q5, Q11, Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 respectively) — the roundtrip holds in both directions.
- **ID continuity is clean.** FR-1..FR-24 contiguous with no gaps or duplicates; UJ-1..UJ-4; SM-1..SM-5 and SM-C1..SM-C4; NFR-1..NFR-7; R-1..R-7. Every cross-reference I checked resolves to an existing ID.
- **SM-2's validation list is redundant** (§11) — "Validates FR-7..FR-12, FR-9, FR-20" lists FR-9 twice, since the range already contains it. Harmless, but if the range was meant to be narrower the duplicate is a symptom.
- **Glossary drift is minimal.** Three lowercase "rail" instances (§2.3 UJ-1 "on a rail rather than dumping him into free flight"; FR-19 "the default experience is on-rail") against the defined term **Rail**. In UJ-1 the lowercase reads as prose and is arguably deliberate; in FR-19's consequence it is a requirement using an undefined-case term. `notable` (code-formatted field) vs **Notable** (Glossary entity) are used distinctly and correctly throughout — that is discipline, not drift.
- **`built` is used in FR-7's schema but never defined** — not in the Glossary, not in any FR. See the duration finding above.
- **`Focus`/`Focused`/`unfocused` are used consistently** with the Glossary definition; no synonym for it appears anywhere ("selected," "active," "hovered" are all absent), which is the discipline §3 asks for.
- **Required sections for launch stakes are all present** — Vision, Target User, Glossary, Features/FRs, NFRs, Non-Goals, Scope, Milestones, Risks, Success Metrics with counter-metrics, Rejected Alternatives, Open Questions, Assumptions Index. Nothing structural is missing.
