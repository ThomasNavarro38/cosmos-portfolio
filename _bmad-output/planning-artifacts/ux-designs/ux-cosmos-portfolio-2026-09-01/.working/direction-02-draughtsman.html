Direction 02

Draughtsman

The whole universe is inverted onto warm paper and drawn as a plate from a technical folio — ink on stock, with a confident and properly graded line rather than a uniform hairline. There is no accent colour anywhere: emphasis is carried entirely by stroke weight, size and the density of the fill, which is how a draughtsman has always done it and which makes the direction immune to a bad monitor. Prose sets in an editorial serif at generous size, so the notable reads like a page from a well-made book while the plate labels stay clipped and technical.

Plate I The field, at rest Opening frame · first paint

Weight does the work that colour does elsewhere. The subject star carries a 2.6px circumference and a heavy 45° hatch; each receding system drops a step in both stroke and hatch density, so depth is drawn rather than lit. The title block anchors the composition and tells the visitor, in the first frame, that this is a document with an author.

Plate II SNCF, system framed Client stop · all projects selectable

Selection is a reticle of four corner brackets plus a step up in circumference weight — a mark from the drawing board, legible with no colour available at all. Deep and Shallow remain a matter of whether a satellite was drawn, and the key states both conventions inside the plate rather than in surrounding chrome.

Plate III The notable, opened Information layer · Deep Project
SNCF · Deep entry

Client referentialMDM replacement

The Oracle MDM cost twice: an expensive licence, and a team employed full-time to correct the data it produced. Replacing it meant rebuilding a mature product's behaviour with no specification to work from — documentation was absent or years stale, so the incumbent's real rules had to be recovered from the system itself.

The house standard was Spring on a dedicated external Tomcat, on ageing on-premise infrastructure. I argued for Spring Boot with embedded Tomcat instead — the better fit, and the furthest the client would go. With a mostly junior team on a critical system, a from-scratch rebuild on a current stack was also the one chance they'd get to learn one.
