Direction 03

Deep Field

This direction spends almost nothing. One uniform hairline draws every orbit and every body, type sits at nine and thirteen pixels, and annotation is stripped to a single label per object — no graticule, no ticks, no console chrome, no frame furniture at all. What is left is emptiness, which is the actual subject: mass and distance read because there is so much nothing around them, and the eye finds the focused object because it is the only white thing on screen.

A — Opening frame

Two lines of chrome in the whole frame, in opposite corners. Everything else is object, orbit and emptiness. White is spent once — on the star the camera is heading for — so the eye lands there before it has read a single word, and the four receding stars step down through grey without ever needing a label to explain that they are further away.

B — Client stop

Selectability is one concentric hairline ring and a step up to white — the least mark that will still carry, and the one the direction can afford. Deep entries keep their satellite; Shallow ones simply have none. Six words of microcopy in the lower right explain the ring once, and then the frame is silent again.

C — Information layer · Deep
SNCF

Client referential — MDM replacement

The Oracle MDM cost twice: an expensive licence, and a team employed full-time to correct the data it produced. Replacing it meant rebuilding a mature product's behaviour with no specification to work from — documentation was absent or years stale, so the incumbent's real rules had to be recovered from the system itself.

The house standard was Spring on a dedicated external Tomcat, on ageing on-premise infrastructure. I argued for Spring Boot with embedded Tomcat instead — the better fit, and the furthest the client would go. With a mostly junior team on a critical system, a from-scratch rebuild on a current stack was also the one chance they'd get to learn one.
