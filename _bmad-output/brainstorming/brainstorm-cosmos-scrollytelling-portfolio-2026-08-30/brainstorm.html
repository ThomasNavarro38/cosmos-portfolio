Technique 01Assumption Reversal · 42 ideas

Every default, turned over.

The session opened by naming what a portfolio site is supposed to do — then refusing each assumption in turn. Four of the reversals survived to become structural commitments.

Time runs forward. A career reads oldest to newest.
Time runs BACKWARD. Scrolling down reverses time — present to past. You meet the person as they are now, then travel outward through what made them. by user
Free exploration is the generous choice — let the visitor roam.
A guided trajectory on rails by default. Free flight exists, but only on request. Curation is the gift; wandering is the escape hatch. by user
Each phase links to the next through a wormhole, ending in a contact form bolted on.
After the reversed big bang the system collapses into a black hole — and that black hole becomes the contact form. The ending isn't appended; it's where all the mass ends up. by user
"Realistic" means astronomically accurate — true scale, true distance.
Realistic means textures, lighting, material quality. Believable to the eye, not to an astronomer. Real distances were explicitly dropped. by user
A collapsing universe should feel dramatic, ominous, final.
The emotional arc is awe, then calm. The universe dies and it feels peaceful. Not violent. Not ominous. by user

Also fixed here, before the reversals began: three phases — education, professional, contact by user · a JSON data structure embedded directly in the project, no CMS and no backend by user · gravity-driven pacing kept as nice-to-have, contingent on complexity staying low by user.

Technique 02Swarm Logic · 80 ideas cumulative

No layout. Only rules.

Nothing in the cosmos gets placed. Every body finds its own position by obeying a small set of local rules — which is also what makes the site maintainable by someone who admits he will not maintain it.

  1. Force-directed layout Positions emerge from attraction and repulsion between entries. Nothing is authored by coordinate. by coach
  2. Mass-damped scroll Heavier bodies resist. Scroll velocity is felt differently depending on what you are passing. by coach
  3. Disable repulsion to collapse The ending is not a separate scene. Switch off one force and the same simulation falls inward on its own. by coach

Those three rules were named by the coach as the spine of the system — one simulation serving layout, pacing, and finale.

The remark that turned out to be architecture by user

Odd items — certs, talks, languages, OSS, gaps — are meteors. Transient objects, not placed bodies.

Said offhand. It became the schema. The coach's reading: absence of fields classifies. No parent and no duration means transient — a meteor. Nothing needs a type flag, nothing needs special-casing, and a new kind of entry can arrive years later without touching the renderer. This is the mechanism that makes the document living, and the synthesis later named it the single most consequential moment of the session.

ONE KNOB. An entry, plus a single weight / relevance number. No position overrides at all — Thomas self-identifies as lazy about maintenance, and overrides would rot the system from the inside. by user

Technique 04Values Archaeology · digging downward

What is this actually for?

The last technique dug beneath the mechanics to find the value the whole thing serves — and corrected a misreading that had quietly shaped the session up to that point.

Surface stratum — the artifact

A cosmos you scroll through. Bodies, rails, textures, a collapse.

Mid stratum — the assumed motive

The coach had been reading the project as inward-facing — a personal monument, an exercise in craft for its own sake. correction by coach

Bedrock — the core value

The site is for others. So they know what Thomas worked on and what his skills are — delivered in a fun way, with awe. Not introspective. Communication is the job; awe is the delivery mechanism. by user

The consequence was immediate and binding: content legibility — the skills, the work, plainly readable — became a hard requirement standing alongside awe, not beneath it. A readable information layer entered the MUST list on the strength of this dig alone.

Build orderM1 → M5 · accepted by user

Greybox first. No vertical slice.

The coach's open question after MoSCoW was what milestone one should contain. The answer was to go straight into it — structure before beauty, deliberately.

  1. M1
    Greybox
    Schema, force-directed layout, rails, backward scroll. The system moving correctly before it looks like anything.
  2. M2
    Information layer
    The legibility requirement made real — skills and work readable, per the core value.
  3. M3
    Realism pass
    NASA texture maps, drei built-ins, post-processing film artifacts. Bought and baked, not modelled.
  4. M4
    Collapse ending
    Repulsion off, gravity in. Pre-rendered Blender bookend. The black hole becomes the contact form.
  5. M5
    Fun layer
    Free flight, nebula gaps, meteors, mass damping, date readout — everything that earns delight once the spine holds.

Four observations, one direction.

01  The offhand "meteor" remark was the schema architecture. Classification by field-absence is what makes the document living.
02  Backward time is one decision serving three masters at once: aesthetic conceit, recruiter-correct ordering, and a non-arbitrary home for the contact form.
03  The admitted 3D skill gap sits in a discipline this project barely uses — space needs spheres, textures and light, all of it free and public-domain.
04  Choosing calm over drama defused the ominous-black-hole risk before anyone had named it as a risk.
